# Maximum ambiguity at binary word lengths 3p−1
**Private specialist review note • 18 November 2026.** Proposed statement and proof; correctness and priority are separate review questions.

For a binary word s, let C(s) be the multiset of (#0,#1) over all its nonempty contiguous substrings. Set E_n=max over length-n words s of the number of distinct words t satisfying C(t)=C(s). Reversals count individually when distinct.

**Proposed theorem.** For every prime p≥5, E_(3p−1)=4.

**Published inputs.** Use the numbering in Acharya, Das, Milenkovic, Orlitsky and Pan, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 29 (2015), 1340–1371. Encode a word by P_s, the sum of x^a y^b over its prefixes including the empty prefix, with (a,b) their compositions. Normalize integer irreducible factors to constant term 1. Write H*=x^(deg_x H)y^(deg_y H)H(1/x,1/y). Thm 12 allows any factors to be replaced by their reciprocals and gives precisely the polynomials of the words of the class. Cor. 18 gives class size 2^r for r nonreciprocal irreducible factors. Lemma 20 controls factor count under diagonal specialization. Thm 22 reconstructs prime-minus-one length up to reversal. Thm 26 describes the class of an interleaving from the classes of its component words. Thm 27 gives E_(3p−1)≥4. [Published source](https://doi.org/10.1137/140962486)

**Proof draft.** Fix n=3p−1. Every P_s(t,t) equals (t^(3p)−1)/(t−1)=Φ₃ Φ_p Φ_(3p), a product of three distinct irreducible cyclotomic polynomials. Every nonconstant factor retains its degree on the diagonal: P_s attains both coordinate degree maxima in its endpoint monomial, hence total degree equals the sum of its coordinate degrees. Degrees add under products, so equality holds for each factor, which consequently has a unique top-degree monomial. Thus a class larger than four must have exactly eight members, with three nonreciprocal irreducible factors. One factor H specializes to Φ₃=1+t+t².

If H is univariate it is reciprocal. Otherwise degree two and the coordinate degree equality force bidegree (1,1), so H=1+a x+(1−a)y+xy, a integral. Write P_s=HQ. By Thm 12, H*Q is also a word polynomial. At x (the degree-one monomial), its coefficient and that of HQ differ in absolute value by |2a−1| since Q(0,0)=1. Both coefficients are in {0,1}. Thus a is 0 or 1. Set F=1+x+xy and G=1+y+xy=F*. Exchanging s with its flipped mate if needed gives two words with P_s G=P_h F.

**Rigidity.** For words of equal length 3m+2 satisfying that identity, compare homogeneous components. Write X_0=x, X_1=y. Degree one gives X_(s1)+y=X_(h1)+x, forcing first bits 0 and 1. Degree two then gives x X_(s2)=y X_(h2), forcing second bits 1 and 0. For m=0 we are done. Otherwise write s=01 c u and h=10 d v for single bits c,d. Their prefix polynomials are F+xy X_c P_u and G+xy X_d P_v. Cancel FG and xy in the identity. Its lowest-degree terms force X_c=X_d; cancelling that common monomial gives P_u G=P_v F. Induction on m proves s=01 b₁ 01 ... b_m 01 with the same separators in h and opposite block orientation.

Here m=p−1. By Thm 26 the entire class of s consists of interleavings of a word equivalent to 01 with a word equivalent to its separator sequence. There are at most two choices for each, the latter by Thm 22. This contradicts eight. The published lower bound gives the claimed equality. ∎

**Optional independent check of inheritance.** Theorem 12 puts F or G in every member; apply rigidity to each. In either orientation, separator number i lies at index 3i+2 counting from zero. For 1≤k≤m every window of length 3k starts at 3j+r for 0≤j≤m−k and 0≤r≤2. It contains separators j through j+k−1, plus k fixed zeros and k fixed ones. Each separator interval occurs exactly three times. Thus its weight-w histogram at length k is one third the full length-3k histogram at weight k+w. The separator record is common to the class, giving the same bound without Thm 26.

**Specific verification requests.** Check the saturated specialization, legitimacy of flipping H over Z[x,y], and the induction for all m including its final block. Audit the indexing separately if using the optional route. The formalization priority is the polynomial-to-word identity and induction with actual word and polynomial types. Existing Z3 checks cover translated arithmetic only. No additional finite search is logically required for this proof; further tests are useful for finding transcription or encoding errors. For priority, determine whether an existing path-polynomial divisibility theorem implies rigidity, or a stronger bound already covers all lengths 3p−1. A known result for interleavings constructed in advance is insufficient by itself.
