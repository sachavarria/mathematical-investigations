# Binary substring compositions at lengths 3p−1
**Independent verification draft 1 • 18 November 2026.** Proposed statement: for every prime p≥5, the largest class of equicomposable binary words of length 3p−1 has exactly four elements. Reversal counts as a different word when it changes the word. Independent review of the complete argument and priority determination are pending.

## Definitions and standing assumptions
A word is a finite sequence over {0,1}. Its record is the multiset of pairs (number of zeros, number of ones) of all nonempty contiguous substrings, including the whole word. Locations are forgotten and multiplicities retained. Write E(s) for all words with the same record as s, and E_n for the maximum of |E(s)| over words of length n. Define P_s(x,y) as the sum of x^a y^b over all prefixes including the empty prefix, where a,b count zeros and ones. Let M_s denote its endpoint monomial. For polynomials H with the endpoint property, H* = x^(deg_x H)y^(deg_y H)H(1/x,1/y). Work in Z[x,y], a unique factorization domain and integral domain. Normalize factors to have constant coefficient one, possible since P_s has constant coefficient one. The polynomial identifies the word: at each total degree its one monomial gives the prefix endpoint. Incrementing the zero exponent means bit 0; otherwise the bit is 1.

## Proof details for verification
**D1.** P_s(t,t)=1+t+...+t^(3p−1), because every prefix length occurs once. By A1 this polynomial has exactly three distinct irreducible factors. P2 ensures each nonconstant bivariate factor uses at least one of them. P1 gives at least three nonreciprocal factors if eight members exist. There are therefore exactly three irreducibles, all nonreciprocal. Each uses one distinct diagonal irreducible, with sign fixed by its constant term. This proves D1. The same upper bound of three factors applies to every word of this length.

**D2.** The endpoint monomial attains both coordinate maxima, hence total degree equals the sum of the two coordinate degrees in P_s. By A2 and the inequality total degree ≤ sum of coordinate degrees, equality must hold in both H and its cofactor. A polynomial attaining that equality has a unique monomial of top total degree; thus its diagonal has the same degree. For H the total degree is two. Bidegrees (2,0) and (0,2) give reciprocal univariates. Bidegree (1,1) gives H=1+a x+(1−a)y+xy, a an integer. The x coefficients of HQ and H*Q differ by 2a−1, since Q has constant 1. P1 makes both products paths (for a product of several irreducibles flip them all). Each path has x coefficient either zero or one. Thus |2a−1|≤1, so a∈{0,1}. Alternatively 1+a x divides P_s(x,0)=1+...+x^j where j counts initial zeros. If a≠0 its root must have absolute value one. Likewise 1−a is zero or has absolute value one on the other axis. The two restrictions again force a∈{0,1}. No assumption on coefficient signs of intermediate factors is needed.

**D3, independent induction.** Write X_0=x and X_1=y. Compare homogeneous degrees in P_s G=P_h F. At degree one the identity reads X_(s1)+y=X_(h1)+x. It forces first bits 0 and 1 respectively. At degree two the cross terms xy and the constant-times-quadratic terms xy are identical on both sides. This leaves x X_(s2)=y X_(h2), forcing second bits 1 and 0. If m=0, both words end here. If m>0 write s=01 c u and h=10 d v, so u,v have length 3(m−1)+2. Splitting off prefixes of lengths 0,1,2 gives
P_s=F+xy X_c P_u and P_h=G+xy X_d P_v.
Substitution cancels FG, leaving X_c P_u G=X_d P_v F. Its lowest-degree terms are X_c and X_d, so the separator bits are the same. Cancel the common monomial. The remaining equality is P_u G=P_v F, exactly the induction hypothesis on the shorter words. This proves D3 for all m≥0. The induction neither assumes nonnegative cofactor coefficients nor ever constructs a quotient.

**D4.** If P_s=FQ, P1 supplies a word h with P_h=GQ. Then P_s G=P_h F. Both have equal length (every class member includes the same whole-word observation), so D3 applies. Exchange s,h for the other orientation. Length 3p−1 equals 3(p−1)+2.

**D5.** Use indices 0 through 3m+1. In a word with copies of a fixed 01 or 10, separator number i lies at index 3i+2, 0≤i<m. Fix 1≤k≤m. Every legal start for a length-3k window is uniquely 3j+r for r=0,1,2, with 0≤j≤m−k. The separator indices in that window are precisely j,...,j+k−1, for all three choices of r. Each residue class of positions occurs k times in a window of 3k positions, so the fixed bits additionally contribute k zeros and k ones. Thus the number of separator windows of length k and weight w is one third of the number of full windows of length 3k and weight k+w. Length and weight determine binary composition. This recovers every required entry. For m=0 there are no nonempty separator windows.

**D6.** Choose a hypothesized class of eight. By D1,D2 and P1 each member contains F or G as a factor: choose the quadratic factor of any representative and follow its orientation in all factor choices of Theorem 12. By D4 every member has one of the two block orientations. D5 gives one common separator record. Each separator has length p−1, hence P3 gives at most two separator words in total. Each choice of separator and orientation spells exactly one full word. This bounds the class by four, a contradiction. Finally apply P1 and P4 as in the register. No generalization to arbitrary mixed primes is assumed.

## Independent checks and limits

Earlier root-of-unity and first-coefficient checks for D2 are independent after the bidegree reduction. The window decoder bypasses polynomial cancellation, while published Theorem 26 provides a separate check once block structure is known.
