# Binary colex ranking by first returns to a minimum factor

Working proof, 26 November 2026. Candidate contribution pending independent verification and confirmation of publication priority. Focus: the binary case. We obtain a subexponential exact method, not a polynomial-time ranking algorithm. No complexity lower bound for ranking is implied.

## Statement

Given an n-bit threshold a, compute all three strict colex prefix counts: the number of necklace representatives before a, the number of primitive representatives before a, and the sum of the least periods of the representatives before a. Each representative is the lexicographically least rotation of its orbit. The algorithm takes 2^{O(sqrt(n log(n+1)))} bit operations and at most that much space. Together with the previously archived worst-case linear-distance reduction from any Grandmama window to a primitive boundary, this gives the same time bound for binary Grandmama temporal ranking. Unranking representatives follows by binary search on reversed numerical keys with O(n) rank calls and a terminal sentinel total. The temporal decoder and unranking are consequences; the reference code tests the prefix counters.

## Construction

Choose a factor length h between 1 and n. Enumerate each possible h-bit string u. We count precisely the representatives whose first h-bit factor is u; in those words u is the least cyclic h-bit factor. For a proposed word w with its zeroth position marked, mark every starting position where the cyclic h-bit factor equals u. List, in forward order, the positive cyclic distances between successive marks, beginning at zero. They sum to n and include the wraparound gap; a single mark has distance n back to itself.

Read the h symbols of u starting at the initial mark. Now, for each gap d, read the next d bits, from the end of this occurrence of u up to and including the end of the next occurrence. Call them the return payload. These payloads concatenate to the cyclic word viewed h places after its original start. If payloads have total length n, their stream bit at offset t occupies position (h+t) mod n of w. This shift is essential for colex comparisons.

**Short-gap rigidity.** Let p be the least finite-word period of u, with no requirement that p divide h. If consecutive occurrences are separated by d<h/2, then d is a period of u. Fine–Wilf applied to the periods p≤d and d shows that gcd(p,d) is a period, hence p divides d. The concatenation of these overlapping copies is p-periodic: their common phase agrees because p divides d. If d>p an occurrence would start p positions after the first, contradicting consecutiveness. Thus d=p. A return of this short kind has the fixed payload consisting of the final p symbols of u. For d=h/2 we use the long branch. Every other gap has length at least h/2, so their number E is at most 2n/h.

A gap schedule is specified by an ordered list of E long lengths and E+1 counts of successive short gaps before, between and after these lengths. The origin is fixed, even if it lies inside a cyclic cluster of short gaps. Each entry is at most n. There are at most n^{O(1+n/h)} schedules including search prefixes, and they can be generated without materializing the entire list. A short gap is permitted only if 2p<h. Impossible schedules subsequently get count zero. For h=1 all gaps enter the long branch.

**Local language.** Summarize a left context by q, the length of its longest suffix which is a prefix of u (including the full length). Its shorter suffix-prefix matches are precisely those prefix lengths k<h for which u[:q] ends in u[:k]. Disallow a next bit b whenever b<u[k] at any such match: that comparison proves the h-factor at that starting point is too small, regardless of its remaining bits. Otherwise update q to the longest prefix of u that is a suffix of u[:q]+b. State h reports completion of an occurrence of u. A length-d payload is read starting at h, following these allowed transitions, avoiding h at internal steps and reaching h exactly on its last bit. This recognizes first returns, with the correct context even for overlapping occurrences. Given complete accepted payloads, this detector returns to h after the concatenation, and the n bits read fully determine its terminal prefix-match state because n≥h. Repeating the accepted concatenation therefore supplies a consistent cyclic context. Every small-factor witness is rejected somewhere on that closed walk. No extra wraparound tests or choices of an initial context are needed.

**Ordering.** The set of allowed first-return payloads from h is prefix-free: an accepted proper prefix of a longer purported payload would already have returned to h. The lex order of two infinite streams of payloads is determined at the first differing payload by its ordinary finite lex order. Among starts of w only the marked ones can be lexically least, since the others begin with an h-factor greater than u. At every marked start the same u precedes its stream of return payloads. Consequently w at origin zero is canonical precisely when its cyclic sequence of payloads is lexically least among all rotations of that sequence. Comparing infinite periodic sequences and comparing equal-length rotations of w give the same outcome. If the resulting cyclic sequence of payload labels has shortest symbol period s, the least bit period of w is the total bit length of the first s payloads. Every bit period fixes the factor at zero and thus carries zero to a mark. Conversely, a rotation fixing the payload-label sequence fixes all actual payloads, hence the bitstream. This also handles nonprimitive representatives, for which counting all tied origins separately would give an incorrect weight.

**Counting all payload assignments for one schedule.** Treat each of its E long payloads as a separate unknown string. Include one additional, known string if short payloads occur; their positions in the schedule always reuse that one string. The length and stream origin of each slot are now given. Read these strings synchronously, in columns, padding completed strings by an end symbol smaller than either bit. Maintain their local detector states and their weak lex order so far. On appending one bit to every active slot, sort their (previous-order-class, new-symbol) pairs and replace the distinct pairs with consecutive integers. This updates the relative lexical order, including equal strings. It never stores the actual prefixes. Bits for the shared short word are predetermined; check its return condition once and include it in the order update. For the colex threshold record the largest original bit position encountered so far where w differs from a, together with the direction of that difference. Each column update handles all its bits using the original positions (h+t) mod n. Fixed short bits can be included before the column scan. Positions are assigned disjointly, so visitation order does not matter.

Use an integer count of partial assignments per state. At the last column reject equal-or-greater thresholds and nonminimal label sequences. For each remaining state with count c, add c to the necklace total, c to the Lyndon total precisely when bit period equals n, and c times the bit period to the weighted total. At fixed u and anchored schedule every accepting assignment determines a single word. Conversely a canonical word uniquely determines its prefix u, its actual marks and the payloads read from its fixed origin, even if rotating by its period yields that same word.

## Cost and outstanding obstruction

At a fixed column the E local states have at most (h+1)^E possible tuples. The weak order is represented by at most E+1 class numbers each at most E+1. The threshold comparison adds at most 2n+1 possibilities. There are at most 2^E outgoing choices per state and at most n columns. Processing polynomial-size per-state data and integers with O(n+log n) bits therefore costs n^{O(1+n/h)} per schedule, and the schedule count is bounded the same way. Summing over all 2^h factors costs 2^{O(h+(1+n/h)log(n+1))}. Choosing h around sqrt(n log(n+1)) proves the claimed bound. It removes the old linear exponent, but no value of h makes both budget terms logarithmic in n. Functional operation counts for individual small instances should not be mistaken for benchmarks against tuned generators.

The state count is an upper bound, not proof that all these states occur on one input. Exact counts for local quasinecklace generations or for all first-return payloads of each length do not by themselves determine their joint order at the selected marked origin. A polynomial algorithm based on this route needs a way to sum over the minimum contexts and/or the order types of return words without explicit replication of all their synchronized detector states. For example, polynomial-time conditioned return-sequence ranking coupled with an aggregation over possible contexts would be meaningful. No impossibility of such a compression has been proved.

## Reproduce

Run python3 return_check.py for an independent finite oracle comparison, and python3 return_rank.py 00010111 for the worked eight-bit threshold. The second and third printed values are unweighted necklace and Lyndon strict counts; the first is the weighted mass. The earlier n=8 weighted answer 171 follows by an independent algorithm here. return_checks.csv records the tested coverage. The verifier first counts the orbit members of every canonical rotation by direct enumeration, then sums all three masses in literal colex order. Its factor lengths include extreme values in the smallest exhaustive cases.

Prior colex generation and the explicit open problem: Daniel Gabrić and Joe Sawada, *Necklaces and Lyndon words in colexicographic order*, arXiv:2607.05324v1, especially §6. For a related fixed-pattern automaton in the lex ranking setting see Tomasz Kociumaka, Jakub Radoszewski and Wojciech Rytter, *Efficient Ranking of Lyndon Words and Decoding Lexicographically Minimal de Bruijn Sequence*, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 30 (2016), DOI 10.1137/15M1043248. The present construction is a preliminary binary first-return approach; it is not a claim to solve the polynomial-time open problem or to have established publication priority.
