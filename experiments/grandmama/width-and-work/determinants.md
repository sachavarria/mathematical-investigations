# Evaluating connectivity and a prescribed past in the same transfer

Candidate derivation and internal audit by Codex, 27 November 2026. The earlier pair-cell determinant sums many columns once all pair quotas are known. Its proof identifies the cost of covering every column by such cells. The calculation here sums over the quota choices as the column is constructed. The price is a signed table indexed by the colors visible at a cut.

## Which columns count?

Let b be a binary column of length n≥1 with z zeros. Write P for the stable permutation that takes the first zero to row zero, the second zero to row one, and so on, and the first one to row z. A primitive rotation packet corresponds to one column whose permutation consists of a single cycle. Starting at row zero and reading the edge labels b along that cycle gives the least rotation backwards.

Given a binary string s of length k≤n, count the columns for which this reading begins with s. Impose a crossing cap D if desired. The count with the cap removed is obtained by setting D=min(z,n−z). Signed weights may be negative at an intermediate stage. The final count is a nonnegative integer.

## Why a color can replace a path matching

Fix the column for a moment. Color a union S of permutation cycles outside the cycle of row zero. Selecting all vertices of one such cycle of length h multiplies its sign by (−1)^h times the sign (−1)^(h−1) of the cycle permutation. Their product is −1. Summing independently over the choice of each external cycle gives zero whenever the column has an external cycle. For a single-cycle column the empty choice contributes one. In determinant notation this is the expansion of the root-deleted cofactor of I−P into principal minors, with term (−1)^|S| sign(P restricted to S). The permutation matrix can use the row-source convention. Its principal minor vanishes unless P maps S onto itself.

An additional color records progress along the prescribed past. Split row zero conceptually into an initial source and a final sink. Send color 0 from the source. At an ordinary vertex in state j<k, require the outgoing bit to equal the next symbol of s, then send j+1 to its target. State k accepts and propagates itself on either bit. Require the sink to receive k. At any external cycle the only compatible ordinary coloring is constant k: a state below k must strictly increase before it can repeat, or encounter a forbidden edge. A selected external cycle instead uses the single color k+1 everywhere. These choices give the same cancellation as before, now also testing the itinerary of the root cycle. If its length is less than k, the root cycle cannot meet the sink condition. For k=0 the initial and accepting states coincide. This accounts for all complete labelings and proves that the signed sum counts the required columns.

## Computing the sign without storing a permutation

After i source rows containing v zeros, the next target is v on zero and z+i−v on one. Thus equal-bit targets occur in increasing order. On the selected rows an inversion arises exactly when a selected one precedes a selected zero. A single running parity of the number of selected ones suffices. Each selected bit contributes a factor −1 for its selection. On a selected zero there is one additional minus sign if the running parity is odd. A selected one toggles the parity. These rules give exactly the cofactor sign. They enforce the actual stable target sequence even across changes in the pair quotas.

The processed source set is [0,i) and the occupied targets are [0,v)∪[z,z+i−v). At a row present in both sets, the incoming and outgoing colors have already been checked for consistency. At a row present in neither set, there is still no color to remember. Retain the colors only on the symmetric difference, omitting row zero because both its roles have fixed boundary conditions. On the next edge, reuse a previously assigned color or try each source color if the source is new. Compute the color arriving at the target, check any earlier assignment there, and remove a color when both roles have been processed. This also handles a loop: the two roles are tested against each other in the same step. Sum the signed weights of identical new states. A completed assignment is generated exactly once by this rule, since an unassigned source is the only place where its color is chosen freely and a newly reached target receives a forced color. With row zero split, all colors off the boundary can be summed out permanently.

The symmetric difference has size 2d, where d=min(i,z)−v. This matters for cost. At each i, v has at most D+1 values surviving the cap. There are at most 2(D+1)(k+2)^(2D) states in the layer, including the inversion parity. There are two choices of next bit and at most k+2 choices of source color. Consequently the work is bounded by poly(n)·(k+2)^(2D+1) and the storage by poly(n)·(k+2)^(2D). Intermediate weights have polynomial bit length: the number of partial assignments is at most 2^n(k+2)^n. For fixed k the bound is singly exponential in D and removes the factorial from the earlier explicit matching table. There is no universal ordering of the sizes of the two tables on particular inputs.

## One evaluation for a complete cutoff

Replace the prefix test with a comparison to the p-bit expansion of a strict upper cutoff. From a currently equal prefix, a smaller next bit leads directly to the accepting state. A larger bit is forbidden. An equal bit moves on to the next comparison except at the last position, where equality is forbidden as well. The same proof applies because all transient states strictly increase and the accepting state alone permits repetition. Cutoffs zero and 2^p mean the empty and full population respectively. Hence one evaluation suffices per primitive length and density, without a separate determinant or transfer for every first-difference position.

For a full population of length n and fixed z, a primitive root of length p dividing n is eligible only when n/p divides z. A root contributes p distinct rooted words. Its reversed key is multiplied by (2^n−1)/(2^p−1) under repetition, yielding the rescaled strict cutoff by ceiling division. Apply the comparator at each eligible divisor and sum the p-weighted counts. With a cap this filters by the crossing width of the primitive packet. An individual selected window's width does not impose that cap on the full population. For the full-length comparison the complexity bound has n+2 in place of k+2. This gives polynomial work on each fixed-width slice; it does not give a general polynomial algorithm or improve the archived unrestricted subexponential estimate.

## A surviving limitation

Even the empty question need not give a small table in this implementation. At n=2m, z=m, m≥2, take the partial column consisting of m−1 ones. Its edges send i to m+i for i=0,…,m−2. Every source except row zero can take either of the two colors when k=0. Their assignments appear separately on the frontier, giving 2^(m−2) nonzero states at this single (i,v). No other prefix reaches v=0 at this i. The prefix really does extend to a single-cycle column under the cap D=m−1: use 1^(m−1)0^(m−1)10. For 0≤j<m−2 the cycle takes j to m+j and then to j+1. It closes via m−2, 2m−2, 2m−1, m−1, 0. Thus the family lies inside the domain of the counting problem. This is a limitation of this explicit signed table. Summing boundary assignments analytically, changing row order, or exploiting a special query can avoid its state count.

## Evidence and context

The oracle enumerates rooted binary strings and keeps only strict lex minima of their nontrivial rotations for primitive-cylinder counts. A separate oracle for weighted ranks directly bins *all* rooted words by the reversal key of their least rotation and by the circular transition count of their primitive root. All binary prefixes, valid zero counts and relevant caps were checked through n=7 (8,291 queries); all strict cutoffs including both endpoints were checked in the same orders for the complete weighted rank (4,249 queries). Twelve additional seeded width-two complete queries at orders 8, 10, 13, 16, 20 and 24 agree with the separate run-length order-type algorithm. These tests cover the stated finite ranges; the mathematical argument supplies the result for other lengths.

At n=64, z=32, D=2, s=101, the answer is one. The signed transfer's largest nonzero layer has 10 states versus 31 for the matching transfer. At n=12, z=6, D=6 with the same query, the answer is 25, and the respective peaks are 2,420 and 154. The switch in which table is smaller is an experimental observation. Neither number is an intrinsic memory requirement. [Reference program](signed_shuffle.py) · [Recorded counts and transition attempts](signed-results.txt)

Determinant dynamic programming for exact connectivity with singly exponential width dependence is established work, including Bodlaender, Cygan, Kratsch and Nederlof, [Solving weighted and counting variants of connectivity problems parameterized by treewidth deterministically in single exponential time](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1505). The present adaptation uses the BWT's two monotone target lists to compute permutation-minor signs with one parity, jointly with the comparison at a distinguished row, while summing over a population of permutations. This specialization requires its own proof. Publication priority has not been established.
