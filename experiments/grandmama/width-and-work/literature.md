# What the width measures, and where the correspondence already appears

Literature and priority audit, 27 November 2026. The comparison uses a binary primitive circle, its last column L, its sorted first column F, and the stable permutation P mapping each occurrence of a letter in L to the same numbered occurrence in F. Length one has crossing width zero. All statements about circular *runs* below concern mixed words.

## A direct antecedent

David Kumallagov explicitly defines the crossing count between the letter blocks of F. Lemma 4.1(b) and Proposition 4.3 of *An extremal problem for completely unclustered Burrows–Wheeler images* identify the number of circular runs of the input with the number of rows where F and L differ. This is stated for general alphabets. In the binary case, writing z for the number of zeros, the number of ones in L before index z equals the number of zeros in L from z onward. Each equals half the Hamming distance between F and L. A mixed binary circle has twice as many circular runs as transitions from one to zero. This establishes directly the row-boundary identity underlying the previous note. [Kumallagov, arXiv:2606.01267, §4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.01267)

The additional maximum-over-cuts step follows by inspecting successive rows. At the cut after i sources let v(i) be the number of zeros already seen. The occupied targets outside the cut number d(i)=min(i,z)−v(i). To the left of z this function stays level or rises, and to the right it stays level or falls. Its maximum is d(z). The identification of binary input morphology with that peak is therefore an elementary corollary of the prior BWT crossing identity. The project-specific question is how to use the peak, or information discarded by taking the peak, to count *other* columns with a prescribed past. The number of runs of L itself is another statistic, prominent in BWT data structures. [Mantaci, Restivo, Rosone and Sciortino, *Burrows–Wheeler Transform and Run-Length Encoding*](https://arpi.unipi.it/bitstream/11568/882901/5/MMRS_WORDS_2017.pdf)

## The same graph has several widths

For a permutation on n rows, exactly d(i) directed arcs leave the first i rows and exactly d(i) enter them. Counting all arcs as undirected edges with their multiplicities gives fixed-layout maximum edge cut 2D. If the permutation is a single cycle of length at least three, the undirected graph is just a cycle. Ordering by traversal gives cutwidth two: every proper consecutive segment has precisely two boundary edges, and the singleton cut attains two. Its pathwidth is also two. Bags consisting of the first vertex and consecutive pairs along the remainder give the upper bound, and a graph of pathwidth one is a forest. Thus rerouting the drawing into cycle order removes large cuts even when D grows. That layout presupposes the successor map of a particular completed column; it provides no fixed online order common to the unknown columns. [Giannopoulou et al., *Cutwidth: Obstructions and Algorithmic Aspects*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-018-0424-7) · [Kinnersley, vertex separation and pathwidth](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(92)90234-M)

The entire profile also has an exact partition interpretation. Draw zero as a northward step and one as an eastward step inside a rectangle of z rows. Let the length of row r, numbered downwards from one, be the number of ones before the (z−r+1)-st zero. After i steps the boundary vertex has coordinates (i−v(i),z−v(i)) in coordinates increasing rightwards and downwards from the top left. The diagonal through this vertex has column minus row equal to i−z. Its number of partition cells is the smaller of the vertex coordinates, namely d(i): moving northwest along the diagonal crosses exactly that many cells before reaching an axis. Hence D is the side of the Durfee square and the sum of d(i) counts the cells, equivalently the number of inversions of L. At fixed z the entire profile determines L: the i-th difference of min(i,z)−d(i) says whether the i-th letter is zero. A faithful picture of the entire cut profile can retain all of the unknown column. Its maximum provides a lossy coordinate with a provable algorithmic use. The Durfee statistic of a binary boundary word and areas of permutation-arc profiles are established objects. [Binary boundary words and the Durfee square, §3](https://math.univ-lyon1.fr/~jouhet/congruences_pams_vf.pdf) · [Guay-Paquet and Petersen, *The generating function for total displacement*, Proposition 3.2](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v21i3p37/pdf/)

## States, transfer matrices and necessity

A transfer matrix moves a vector of partial counts from one cut to the next. A frontier search combines partial graphs sharing enough boundary information for future completions. This principle and endpoint-connectivity states have substantial precedent. The special feature here is the ordered pair of target queues given by stable sorting. At (i,v) their consumed intervals are known, even though the completion is unknown. The archived transfer combines the pairing of the exposed path endpoints with a word summary determined by the requested itinerary. Its factorial term bounds the number of matchings stored by this construction. [Kawahara, Inoue, Iwashita and Minato, frontier-based search](https://doi.org/10.1587/transfun.E100.A.1773)

For a deterministic test that must decide every allowed continuation, two feasible prefixes can share a state exactly when the same suffixes complete them successfully. For a test constrained to store linear combinations over a specified field, the rank of the prefix-by-suffix acceptance matrix lower-bounds the required linear dimension at that cut, by matrix factorization. These are different notions of necessity; neither equals the number of observed or theoretically possible endpoint matchings by definition. Known algorithms for counting graph connectivity exploit algebraic compression. Known conditional lower bounds for counting Hamiltonian cycles use graph families realizing particular matching-compatibility matrices. A common upper bound on endpoints supplies no such realization among LF columns. [Bodlaender, Cygan, Kratsch and Nederlof, rank-based and determinantal dynamic programs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1505) · [Curticapean, Lindzey and Nederlof, connectivity matrices and counting lower bounds](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611975031.70)

## Priority of the ranking and determinant constructions

* Sawada, Williams and Wong already use maximal blocks of zeros followed by ones, represented by two run lengths, in their colex generation algorithm. Section 2 also defines their lexicographic order on these blocks. A counting algorithm using run pairs should credit both components. In the sources checked here the candidate additional result is an arbitrary full-length cut on a population bounded by D, with a polynomial degree independent of D. One construction aggregates weak order types. The sharper variant applies the earlier subset transfer for minority gaps to ordered pair letters and two length budgets, obtaining 6^D times a polynomial in n. [Bounded-width transcript proof](run-transcripts.md) Output-sensitive generation and fixed-density lexicographic ranking solve different tasks. The unrestricted colex decoding question remains. [Sawada, Williams and Wong, §2](https://www.cis.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/neck_brgc.pdf) · [Hartman and Sawada, fixed-density ranking](https://www.cis.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/fixDenRank.pdf) · [Gabrić and Sawada, colex generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05324)
* Fici and Gabory explicitly study primitive BWT images that concatenate alphabet permutations and relate them to Hamiltonian cycles in generalized de Bruijn graphs. In the binary case this includes the cell requiring a zero and a one in every pair. At length 64 its unconditioned count is the classical number of binary de Bruijn cycles of order six, 2^(32−6). The archived conditioned calculation additionally specifies the marked row's itinerary, allows nonuniform binary pair quotas, and contracts the resulting trail. [Fici and Gabory, MFCS 2025, especially §3](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2025.48)
* The determinant that detects a one-cycle permutation by deleting one row and column from I−P is an immediate matrix-tree specialization, explicitly Lemma 20 in Baitian Li's ICALP 2026 paper. That work evaluates a Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial on edge weights, including a data structure with exponential preprocessing. Applying the same connectivity indicator to a stable-shuffle sum still requires enforcing the order of repeated letters and the chosen itinerary. A compact algebraic expression alone gives no polynomial evaluation cost. [Li, *Counting Perfect Matchings and Hamiltonian Cycles Faster*, §6](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2026.138)

## Scope of the search

The search included English-language terms for BWT circular input runs, standard permutations, crossings between letter blocks, first/last-column Hamming distance, permutation arc profiles, Durfee sizes of binary words, graph layout width, frontier search, connectivity-matrix rank, run-length necklace generation and fixed-parameter colex ranking. The direct antecedent above emerged under *cyclic input run number* and *completely unclustered images*. This audit establishes positive attributions where the statements match. No consulted source gave the same restricted weighted colex rank algorithm as the present development. That observation supplies a provisional novelty assessment, pending specialist review, rather than a publication-priority determination.
