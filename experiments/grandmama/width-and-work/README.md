## What the picture retains

The paired rims arrange the same rooted words in different orders. The cut view enters one rotation packet. Every arch follows an actual stable-sort edge from a row to its destination. Moving the cut reveals a prefix of the last column. The bands describe all columns with that prefix and density, including those that will fail the one-cycle test. Band weight records how many completions share that **segment**. An entire profile at fixed density determines the column uniquely: if d(i) is its height, then min(i,z)−d(i) counts the zeros seen by cut i. Successive differences recover every bit. A visual design that placed several complete columns on one identical profile would misrepresent the data.

The profile offers three distinct readings. Its peak D counts the maximum simultaneous one-direction crossings. Its area counts how long those crossings collectively remain open. Its complete sequence of slopes recovers the last column. Thus two scalar summaries lose different information from a picture which, in full, preserves enough to reconstruct the original circle.

For the area identity, sum one unit over each cut crossed by each rightward edge. A one in source row i moves right exactly as many places as there are zeros after it. The sum is therefore the number of inversions of the last column. The permutation sign is (−1)^area. A one-cycle column must have area congruent to n−1 modulo two. At fixed z and peak D, split the column at z. The D left ones invert with all D right zeros. Inversions within the two subwords can add anything from zero up to D(z−D)+D(n−z−D) at the extremes, giving sharp unrestricted endpoints D² and D(n−D). Arranging each subword in increasing or decreasing order realizes those endpoints. Connectivity still matters. At n=7 and z=2 the columns 1110110 and 1111001 both have peak two and area eight. Only the first gives a single cycle. The underlying inversion and diagram interpretations have classical precedents. [Source register](literature.md)

## What must be remembered for a question?

Use the cut view on 00010111. At cut three, the last column begins 100. Ten columns remain at its density. Exactly two form a single cycle. Both give an observable past beginning in 111, and both reject 101. The program can answer either of those questions while the identity of the packet remains undetermined. Its literal enumerator follows 58 successors for each query. That is a count of operations actually performed by this enumerator. It is separate from the ten possibilities, the two connected possibilities, and the resources a better algorithm might require. [Instrument checks](check_scene.cjs) · [Data](scene-results.txt)

There is an exact notion of necessary memory at a specified cut for a specified computational model. Fix the length, density, cut index i, and number of zeros v already consumed. All eligible prefixes have the same set of legal suffixes. For each prefix write down its yes/no answers across those suffixes for the joint condition of one cycle and canonical colex key below K. Two different rows of this table must be distinguished by any deterministic left-to-right membership algorithm: the very same suffix separates their answers. One state per distinct row also suffices at that layer, by classifying prefixes according to their future answers. This counts abstract states in that model with arbitrary precomputation. Computing the table efficiently is a separate issue. A rank algorithm may sum all answers algebraically without implementing such a membership test.

For n=12, z=6, i=6, v=3, every prefix and suffix together give peak three. At integer cuts K=2681 and K=3737, the minimum numbers of distinguishable prefix states are four and nine. At the latter cutoff there are only five different counts of accepting suffixes: storing just that number loses which suffixes can complete the prefix. At the full cutoff K=4096 the minimum falls to seven. The geometry and number of candidate completions are fixed throughout. Changing the question changes the information a sequential observer must retain. [Exact enumeration](state_questions.py) · [All changes of the running maximum](state-results.txt)

## Ranking and review

* The [proof review](review.md) separates logical validity from exposition. It corrects a space-accounting ambiguity, audits periodic boundaries and the determinant lift, and expands the checks of excluded finite cases. [Original evidence and earlier arguments](../morphology-of-order/README.md)
* The [literature register](literature.md) identifies the prior BWT block-crossing theorem, graph layout and pathwidth distinctions, and the antecedents of determinantal and run-based enumeration.
* A [first algorithm based on run-length orders](width-rank.md) makes the full cut fixed-parameter tractable in D alone. A [sharper transfer based on comparison transcripts](run-transcripts.md) reduces this to 6^D poly(n) time and 2^D poly(n) space. It gives polynomial work throughout D≤C log n for each fixed C, for an arbitrary full-length threshold on the restricted population.
* A [signed transfer](determinants.md) sums across pair quotas while respecting the order of equal-bit targets. It uses a boundary coloring and a parity bit in place of an explicit path matching. Its table can still be exponentially large. This does not resolve general exact Grandmama decoding.

The proofs and software are candidate contributions developed and internally audited by Codex. Independent proof verification and confirmation of priority are open. The displayed band weights encode enumerated counts. Responsiveness was checked in browser viewports of 390 × 844 and 844 × 390 CSS pixels. The designs carry no empirical claim about a viewer's experience. One question for use with the draft is whether varying the query against a fixed arrangement makes agreement among unresolved histories perceptible before reading its count.
