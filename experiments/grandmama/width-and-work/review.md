## Correctness

### 1. Width of a stable shuffle

Take a nonempty binary last column of length n with z zeros. Sort its symbols stably, preserving the order of equal symbols. This gives a permutation P from each source position to its position after sorting. At a cut following i source positions count edges whose sources are on the left and whose targets are on the right. All statements here use the row numbers in their given order.

Let v count zeros on the processed side. If i≤z, every processed zero lands on the left, and every processed one lands on the right. There are i−v such ones. If i≥z, all zero targets are on the left and z−v of them are still unoccupied. Since there are i processed sources and i left-side targets, the number of missing left-side targets equals the number of processed edges landing on the right. Thus the crossing count is min(i,z)−v. It increases weakly up to i=z and decreases weakly thereafter. This formula holds for every column. To interpret its maximum on a circular binary word requires the BWT hypothesis: for a primitive circle, its n distinct rotations provide the n rows, and the first z rows begin with zero. A one in their last column records exactly one circular transition into zero. Proper powers carry the count of their primitive root when the rows are deduplicated as in the instrument. The two one-letter circles give zero.

**Counting-state invariant.** In the partial permutation, split the distinguished row into a source and a sink by redirecting the only edge into zero to a fresh sink. Before a directed cycle closes, each nonempty component is a directed path. Its initial vertex is in the processed source set but outside the occupied target set. Its final vertex is in the target set but outside the source set. Both sets are determined by i and v, even though the pairing between them depends on the particular prefix. Redirecting target zero adds at most one possible pair of endpoints. Concatenating paths splices the corresponding endpoint pairings. Closing a directed cycle can safely be rejected because no later edge can enter or leave that component. After the last row, all surviving edges form the single marked source-to-sink path. Conversely splitting any accepted single cycle produces a path that survives every step of this procedure.

The factor summary also needs a compositional invariant. Store the longest initial segment of a path's labels occurring inside the requested query string s, together with a bit indicating that more text followed. Once the left operand has failed, no extension can change its summary. Otherwise the right operand can be read up to its first failure. If that failure character is absent from the stored text, it could not extend a successful combined factor: the offending initial segment of the right operand would itself be a suffix of that factor. This proves the merge rule including the empty query and failure at the very first bit.

At a layer with crossing cap D, v takes at most D+1 values. For each value there are at most D+1 pairs, with at most (D+1)! pairings and M^(D+1) summary assignments for M≤2+k(k+1), where k=|s|. Each state has two branches and integer mass at most 2^n. These are **sufficient states** for this explicit transfer. A lower bound on necessary states would have to exhibit distinguishable futures under the specified input model. The crossing statistic counts directed edges in one direction. The undirected edge count of the same permutation arcs, retaining multiplicity and ignoring loops at the cut, is twice this number because a permutation crosses equally often in both directions. Optimizing over vertex order is a further operation.

**Complete query.** An unrestricted strict cut decomposes into at most n backwards prefixes. A powered root of primitive order p dividing n contributes weight p after the threshold has been rescaled. At fixed density keep only compatible divisors. If a width cap is imposed on the primitive populations, each such summand must observe its own cap. This is a restricted weighted rank. If instead the cap is defined on transitions of the repeated length-n word, the primitive cap becomes floor(D/(n/p)). With no cap promise the transfer must accommodate widths as large as min(z,n−z) even when the packet that supplied the query is narrow. A resource report needs the sum of the transitions processed across layers, prefixes, densities when relevant, and active divisors. Peak states in a single layer measure one aspect of space. Matrix storage, summary construction and memoized merges add costs of their own. The established transfer alone is fixed-parameter tractable in (D,k) and polynomial for fixed D even at full length, with a D-dependent exponent. These qualifications attach to the transfer reviewed here.

### 2. Pair quotas and the last-exit determinant

Condition on the zero count in every adjacent row pair, including a final singleton when necessary. The quota fixes the *set* of outgoing row labels for each pair. At a mixed pair the two targets have distinct bits. Therefore an incoming row and the next requested bit determine at most one outgoing edge. At an unmixed pair stable order already fixes each target, so the two rows must be separate vertices. Each permitted binary filling is a bijection between the incoming and outgoing edges at every vertex of this balanced directed multigraph. It produces a single LF cycle exactly when those bijections form one Euler tour, a closed walk using every labelled edge once.

The observer starts at incoming edge zero and reads the color of its **successor**. A requested prefix forces a single trail starting with that distinguished edge. Reusing any edge too early rules out a full tour. Contract the trail to a marked edge. Expansion gives back the original tour uniquely, even when the trail revisits a vertex. For a prefix of length n the last color must be that of the initial edge. This separates the obligatory return from an illegal early closure. The degree of a vertex cannot increase under contraction: internal arrivals and departures are removed in pairs, and the exposed tail and head receive the new edge. After isolated vertices are discarded, all positive degrees are at most two.

For each vertex other than the root of the circuit, reserve its last exit. In a complete tour these reserved edges lead toward the root: after using the last exit at one nonroot vertex, reaching another nonroot vertex forces a later departure there. A directed cycle of last exits is therefore impossible. Conversely a tree of reserved exits and the prescribed first edge determine all remaining local orders, because each vertex has degree at most two. A prematurely terminated walk would have to end at the root by balance. The unused edges would then also form a balanced remainder. From a vertex with unused edges, its reserved final exit is unused, leading to another vertex with unused edges, ultimately to the exhausted root. This contradiction proves that the walk is complete. Hence a principal minor of the directed outdegree Laplacian counts the compatible tours. Disconnected balanced graphs give zero. A lone active vertex uses the empty minor with value one. Directed loops are kept in the edge walk and cancel in the Laplacian.

**Work accounting.** The input graph has O(n) edges. The reference determinant implementation materializes an O(n²)-entry matrix, with O(n³) arithmetic work per cell and prefix and polynomial bit complexity by bounds on integer minors. Its sparse graph description alone does not bound the working memory of this implementation. The 3^floor(n/2) count of quota choices has an extra factor of two at odd lengths. The explicit positive-cover argument applies even if distinct cells use different disjoint pairings. It bounds cells of that type covering the full population. A symbolic sum over them need not have the running time of that cover.

### 3. Strip relaxation and the symbolic theorem

For each interval and bit, stable sorting fixes a list of raw targets. Splitting the twisted list into contiguous increasing strips within destination intervals retains a known multiplicity for each strip type. Allowing the types to occur arbitrarily at their source enlarges the set of fillings. To recover exactly the binary fillings, retain only arrangements in which the types of each split list appear in their original order after erasing symbols from the other list. Every binary filling gives precisely one retained arrangement. The reverse map reads off each bit. Under alignment the order restriction is automatic.

Traversing a relaxed one-cycle filling gives a cyclic word in strip types. Distinct complete histories at visits to a vertex are ordered by their first differing incoming strip, starting at the most recent visit. Equality of an incoming strip transports the source order to its destination. If histories coincide at two positions, the type circle has a proper period. Following this period on the visits with a common history permutes them increasingly, so it fixes each one. This contradicts the assumed full cycle. Conversely sorting the visits of a primitive type circle by these histories orders every occupied strip as required and reconstructs its single cycle. Thus the relaxed count is a count of primitive cyclic type words. Resolving indistinguishable *copies* of every type multiplies a rooted linear word count by the product of the corresponding factorials, even for periodic words, since the positions in a linear word are fixed. Removing powers by divisor inversion precedes division by the primitive length. Intervals have positive length; a zero-length piece has no edge type. Disconnected graphs cannot produce a single tour. A three-row split example in the original proof demonstrates that relaxing precedence can strictly increase the answer.

The bridge to the original symbolic theorem concerns a **specified monomial**. With the root deleted, let S have a tail indicator per edge and T its signed tail-minus-head incidence column. The weighted tree minor is det(S X Tᵀ), where X has distinct formal edge variables on its diagonal. Introduce new diagonal variables Y. In the square matrix with rows of blocks (0, S Y) and (−X Tᵀ, Y), each x variable occurs at most twice, each y variable at most twice, and there are no nonzero scalar-only entries. A loop has zero incidence. Factoring the y variable out of each right-hand column gives a lower-right identity block. Block elimination with determinant one then gives (product of the y variables) times the tree minor, as a polynomial identity. This proof also holds when a later evaluation sets a y variable to zero.

In particular, for an edge subset A and distinct variables on all edge types, the coefficient of (product of all y) times (product of x in A) is one exactly when A is a spanning tree directed to the root, and zero otherwise, by the directed matrix-tree theorem. We work over the integers for counts and may first take formal coefficients over the rationals to apply the original field statement. To evaluate the total tree count, set all y variables to one and substitute each multiplicity for its x variable. The original coefficient algorithm provides a formally valid specialization of its scope. Ordinary determinant evaluation already performs this *total* sum in polynomial time. The original theorem's three-occurrence hardness result does not transfer through this embedding. [Symbolic research archive](../../../symbolic-determinants/README.md)

## Explanatory clarity

A small population separates the three operations. The primitive circle 001011 has last column 101100. Its first three sorted rows start in zero. Two of them end in one, corresponding to its two circular 1→0 transitions. The one-way crossing profile, including its empty and final cuts, is 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 0. The explicit transfer records endpoint identities at that cut. The count two alone leaves their pairing unspecified.

For an example with actual uncertainty, fix all four pair quotas to one at length eight. There are sixteen binary last columns in this cell. Exactly two have one cycle. Their least rotations are 00010111 and 00011101, giving backwards strings 11101000 and 10111000. A query for the past 10 accepts the second and rejects the first. Within this same cell the two bits force one initial trail. Only its possible *completions* are left for the determinant to count. These examples separate the geometry of one object, knowledge of a restricted family, and the price of choosing enough families to answer a full query.

## What the finite checks establish

The original analytic proofs cover unrestricted finite lengths under their stated assumptions. The experiments below challenge the implementation of their reductions. Exhaustion at a stated order establishes a finite assertion over those inputs.

| Obligation | Scope |
|---|---|
| Frontier transfer versus direct sorted rotations | Original 8,291 comparisons include every binary prefix, zero count and relevant cap through order 7, including empty and full prefixes. |
| Pair cells versus direct sorted rotations | Original coverage is exhaustive through order 6. Orders 7 and 8 originally omitted prefixes absent from all primitive necklaces. The added test evaluates every bit string of length 0 through n against every feasible cell at both orders: 13,770 and 41,391 pairs. The counts include 13,628 and 41,123 zero answers respectively. |
| Primitive fixed-density ranks | The original 768 complete threshold comparisons extend through order 6. This test concerns the primitive contribution. The divisor extension for weighted rank is a separate argument. |
| Strip bound and alignment | Every ordered positive interval composition, compatible quota vector and admissible twist through residual length 7 was tested against direct permutation enumeration. The order-9 follow-up sampled all *positive* pinned layouts at the three stated depths. It gives no frequency over all possible layouts. |
| Symbolic support and coefficients | The new check expands the bordered determinant by signed permutations as a formal polynomial, and independently lists edge subsets forming trees. Coverage is every labelled directed multigraph presented by an ordered list of 0 through 3 edges on 1 through 3 named vertices, loops and repeats included, rooted at vertex zero. There are 909 input graphs and 91,131 Leibniz permutations. Direct coefficient agreement is stronger finite evidence than agreement at selected numerical values. |
