## Conventions

The binary clock of order n ≥ 2 emits the shortest repeating root of each lexicographically least rotation (necklace representative), visiting these length-n representatives in increasing colex order. Colex compares rightmost differing bits first. The first emitted bit has phase zero. A primitive representative contributes n bits and its block start is a recognizable anchor. Its forward window is the representative itself, which occurs only once by the de Bruijn property. Define C(a) for ANY n-bit threshold as the sum of the least periods of representatives strictly below a in colex order. At an anchor this is its absolute phase. At a nonrepresentative it is an insertion weight.

## Exact maximum gap

**Theorem.** If p is the smallest prime divisor of n, the largest cyclic distance between consecutive anchors is n + max(2,n/p). Consequently the longest backward search for an anchor takes n + max(2,n/p) − 1 steps. For the one-anchor cycle of order two its gap is the full circumference.

**Separation lemma.** Between two distinct nonconstant periodic representatives x < y in colex order lies a primitive representative. Let j (one-based) be the first one of y, and i its rightmost difference with x. The bit y_i is one, hence i ≥ j. If i=j, x and y agree after j. The last full copy of the root of y lies after j, since j ≤ period(y) ≤ n/2. That copy contains the leading run of j−1 zeros of y. Since x is its own least rotation, x must begin with at least that many zeros. Thus x and y differ at just j. This is impossible for two proper powers of length n: rotate their single difference to the last position. The common n−1 prefix has periods d and e, their respective least periods. Its length is at least d+e−gcd(d,e), since both periods are at most n/2. By Fine and Wilf it has period gcd(d,e), so its positions n−d and n−e agree and force identical final bits. This proves i>j.

Change the first one of y to zero to form z. Its leading zero run is now strictly longer than any other run, since the leading run was already maximal, and the nonconstant proper power y ends in one and has another copy of its root. Thus z is primitive and is its least rotation. Changing bit j decreases y in colex while preserving its comparison with x at i>j. This gives x<z<y. For example, at order six the periodic words 001001 and 010101 are separated by 000101 obtained from the latter by this operation.

The extreme nonconstant representatives are 0^(n−1)1 and 01^(n−1), both primitive. Indeed any nonconstant representative starts with zero and ends in one; these two have respectively the smallest and largest colex keys subject to those conditions. Thus the two constants form a run of total weight two across the seam. Every other run of short blocks consists of at most one block by the lemma. The largest possible proper divisor is n/p; for d=n/p≥2, repeat the primitive word 0^(d−1)1 to realize a block of that length. Adding the n bits of the preceding anchor proves the theorem, including prime orders.

Given any local window, test for an anchor while walking backward (two candidate predecessors and the published forward successor rule). Let a be the first anchor, j the distance. Phase = C(a)+j modulo 2^n. The theorem makes this a worst-case reduction at every binary order. A phase oracle in turn gives C directly on primitive representatives. This equivalence concerns the restricted anchor queries; the counter below also handles arbitrary thresholds.

## Computing C without a stored clock

**Theorem.** For every binary threshold a, C(a) can be computed in polynomial space and O*(2^ceil(n/2)) time, where O* suppresses polynomial factors in n. The algorithm accepts only a, with no advice table. Together with the gap theorem this gives the same bounds for decoding arbitrary windows. This remains exponential, and the reference Python program prioritizes auditability over speed.

Set h=floor(n/2). Partition canonical words by whether their initial h-bit factor occurs exactly once around the circle.

1. For each of the 2^h possible initial strings u, count words w starting with u whose other cyclic length-h factors all strictly exceed u and whose colex key precedes a. Such w are primitive canonical representatives, each of weight n. The strict comparisons imply both statements, and every canonical word with a unique initial factor satisfies them.
2. In the remaining class there is an offset d in 1,...,n−1 with w[i]=w[(i+d) mod n] for 0≤i<h. For fixed d these h equalities leave exactly n−h free bits. To see this, consider the directed rotation i↦i+d. Its orbits consist of equally spaced residues mod gcd(n,d). No entire orbit fits in the first h positions: the complement is a consecutive interval of length at least n/2 and meets every residue. The h selected edges are therefore disjoint paths, including the possibility of isolated vertices. Each edge removes one free bit. Enumerate the 2^(n−h) component assignments. Keep the word only at its least positive matching offset to prevent double counting. Test canonicality, colex threshold and primitive period directly, and add its period. No list of previously seen words is required.

For completeness, the unique-factor count in step 1 is a polynomial-size dynamic program. Read w followed by u's first h−1 bits to close the circle. Force the known prefix and closing bits, branching on the unknown bits. Store the length q of the longest suffix read which matches a proper prefix of u. All shorter active matches are suffixes of u[:q], including the empty string. At the next bit, each such match of length k gives a start i−k (zero-based current index i). For starts from 1 to n−1, reject a smaller next bit, or an equal bit that completes the full factor. These are precisely the first mismatches witnessing an offending rotation prefix or a tie. Updating q needs only u[:q] and the new bit. A second Boolean records whether the colex comparison is currently strictly less: the latest differing bit overrides the earlier decision, and closing bits do not participate. Sum multiplicities in each state and select the strictly-less states at termination. Counts have O(n) bits. Iterating u and d sequentially proves the space bound and the stated time bound. Splitting off the exceptional class matters: for periodic words, counting one per successful rotation would give the wrong temporal mass.

**Worked boundary.** At n=8 the word 00010111 has phase 171. Using h=4, unique-factor representatives contribute 120 bits and tied-factor representatives 51. This is a count of bits emitted by earlier blocks. The threshold's reversed numerical key is 232.

## What the index buys

The interface executes a different exact algorithm on the same C. Set k between zero and n. The k trailing bits, read right to left, choose a bucket of B=2^(n−k) consecutive keys. Cache the total temporal mass before each bucket, then scan the residual keys in that bucket. If r is the reversed binary key of the observation, the query performs precisely r mod B candidate tests in addition to a table read when k>0. Maximum residual B−1; mean (B−1)/2 for a uniform threshold over ALL n-bit words. A uniform query over anchors need not give that mean. The identity 2^k(B−1+1)=2^n is an exact storage-versus-worst-scan relation for this index family, not a data-structure lower bound.

The object builds the index by scanning all 2^n candidates once per positive depth, storing only the start sums in a Uint32Array (4·2^k bytes for the supported n≤14). Zero depth stores no array and performs no build scan. A cached build is reused across queries at the same order and depth. Indexing, classification of the submitted word, comparisons within a candidate test, transient storage, and display work are distinct from candidate test counts. The two strips depict exact counts in different measures, candidate keys versus emitted temporal bits. Their green segments visualize the work returned by a lookup, and the hatched segments visualize the contribution computed on this query. The renderer needs no global rank table in the background.

For 00010111, depths 0, 2, 4, 6 and 8 require respectively 232, 40, 8, 0 and 0 residual tests. Their saved masses are 0, 95, 155, 171 and 171. After a fresh build, depth 4 spends 256+8=264 build and residual tests on the first query, versus 232 without an index. For two identical queries these totals become 272 and 464 in this comparison (the no-index baseline deliberately has no answer memoization). A query workload concentrated on one word could instead cache a single answer. The cost comparison depends on what the observer will be asked again.

A different build is now possible in principle: compute each bucket start independently by the polynomial-space counter. Storing 2^k such answers costs O(n·2^k) bits with ordinary binary counters and the construction costs O*(2^(k+ceil(n/2))) time. For a small index this improves the asymptotic bound over a complete cycle scan. The interface deliberately uses the scan build to make its operation counts inspectable. Having the same stored representation does not determine how much work it cost to produce.

## Reproduction and limits

Run `python3 check.py` to reproduce results.csv. It constructs an independent oracle by canonicalizing every word and counting members in each orbit. All thresholds at n=2,...,8 and five thresholds per n=9,...,14 agree with the new counter. The gap calculation uses every representative at all these orders. Run `python3 weighted.py 00010111` for the example and `node check-interface.cjs` for the actual HTML script's query and counter checks at all depths and thresholds through order 8. Open Index-Study.html directly in a browser. No network or build step is required. Prior exhaustive gap measurements extend to order 16. No Lean formalization, independent proof review or human timing study is claimed.

**Next experiment.** Keep the query word and index fixed while changing how the same bucket totals are constructed. Then compare observers who see only the absolute coordinate with observers who also see the two partial sums. Can they predict which nearby words reuse a count? Record accuracy and operations separately. The current object provides inspectable quantities and a falsifiable perceptual question, not a measured claim about human attention.

## Literature and priority

- Dragon et al., [Grandmama construction and successor](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2018.03.006).
- Sawada, Williams and Wong, [colex generation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jda.2017.10.002). Constant amortized generation still requires visiting intervening objects if used as a rank method.
- [Necklaces and Lyndon words in colexicographic order](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05324), §6, explicitly identifies efficient colex rank/unrank and the connection to Grandmama decoding as open. That connection predates this note.
- [Practical algorithms to rank necklaces, Lyndon words, and de Bruijn sequences](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1570866717300175) and [decoding project](https://debruijnsequence.org/db/rank) for the lexicographic comparison. Reversing the comparison key does not convert the least rotation into the least reversed rotation in general.
- N. J. Fine and H. S. Wilf, “Uniqueness theorems for periodic functions,” Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 16 (1965), 109–114, for the periodicity lemma.

The proposed contributions here are the exact gap proof and the polynomial-space exponential-time bound for weighted colex prefix counting. The index family uses ordinary bucket prefix sums. Neither priority nor optimality of either mathematical bound has been established.
