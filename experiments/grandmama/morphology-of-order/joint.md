# Conditioning a stable shuffle on its observable past

Working derivation and finite checks by Codex, 27 November 2026. Candidate contribution pending independent proof verification and priority assessment.

## The joint count

At length n, sort the rotations of a primitive binary necklace lexicographically. Write b for the column of last bits and z for its number of zeros. The stable-sort permutation P sends the ith zero of b to the ith row of the first column's zero block, and likewise for ones. Row numbers begin at zero. The primitive BWT image consists of exactly those b for which P is one cycle. Reading b at rows 0, P(0), … spells the least rotation backwards. For a prescribed bit prefix s, the target is the number of columns that satisfy both this itinerary and the one-cycle condition.

Partition the row positions into consecutive pairs with a final singleton when n is odd. A **cell** fixes the zero count in each part. Every column belongs to precisely one cell. Because preceding quotas are fixed, the sets of destination indices assigned to each pair are fixed too. A pair with equal bits has a forced bijection to its two destinations in increasing order. In a mixed pair the two assignments interchange its zero destination and its one destination. Distinct mixed pairs can be chosen independently prior to the one-cycle constraint.

**Proposition.** Within any such cell, the joint count for any s of length at most n can be computed using one uniquely determined partial walk and at most one ordinary integer determinant. The evaluation has polynomial bit complexity in n, independent of the number of possible switch configurations in that cell.

**Construction and proof.** Form a directed multigraph with distinct edge labels 0 through n−1. A mixed pair of rows forms a vertex. Each remaining row forms a vertex on its own. Edge j enters the vertex containing row j. Its tail is the vertex whose assigned targets include j. For an unmixed pair, its two singleton vertices have the two respective fixed targets from stable sorting. Every vertex has equal indegree and outdegree, either one or two. Give edge j color zero if j<z and one otherwise.

A local choice of successor for each incoming edge, bijective onto the outgoing edges, is exactly a choice of the column in the cell. The resulting permutation on edge labels is P. It is a single cycle precisely when these successors form one Euler circuit using all labelled edges. A tour is read with incoming edge 0 distinguished, so the correspondences carry no cyclic multiplicity. The next edge's color is the bit b at the current row. A mixed vertex has one outgoing edge of each color, and a singleton has only one exit. Thus the colors of s uniquely determine the successive edges if such a walk exists.

For k=|s|<n trace the k steps after incoming edge 0. A missing requested color or any repeated edge, including an early return to 0, gives zero. Otherwise the directed trail of k+1 distinct edges starting at edge 0 is forced. Replace it with one distinguished edge from its first tail to its last head, retaining all unused labelled edges and discarding isolated vertices. Expansion and contraction give inverse bijections between cyclic tours subject to the observed itinerary and tours of this reduced graph beginning with the distinguished edge. The trail may revisit a vertex. Distinctness is required only of its edges. Balance survives contraction and positive degrees are at most two. For k=n first require the last requested color to be the color of edge 0, then use k=n−1. This includes the one-row cases and a trail consuming all edges.

For a balanced connected directed multigraph with a prescribed initial edge, the BEST theorem gives the number of tours as an in-arborescence count times the product of (outdegree−1)! over active vertices. Here each factorial equals one. Equivalently, reserving a final outgoing edge at every vertex other than the start specifies a tree toward the start. Once those edges and the initial edge are reserved there is only one local order at every vertex. If the walk stopped before covering all edges, the unused last exits would lead along this tree to the start, contradicting exhaustion at the start. Conversely the final exits of a complete tour form such a tree. The directed matrix-tree theorem evaluates the result by one principal cofactor of the outdegree Laplacian. Loops cancel in the Laplacian; labelled parallel edges are counted separately. A disconnected balanced graph gives zero; a single active vertex uses the empty determinant one. The cofactor can omit any active vertex, since balance makes its row and column sums zero and hence all principal cofactors equal. The construction proves the proposition. [BEST and last-exit trees](https://darijgrinberg.gitlab.io/t/22s/lec16.pdf) · [Directed matrix-tree theorem](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/MatrixTreeTheorem.html)

## Global cost and its limit

A strict colex cut is a disjoint union of at most n prefixes of the reversed key, one at each first difference where the cutoff has bit one. The number of quota cells before restriction to a density is exactly 3^floor(n/2) times 2^(n mod 2). Per prefix and cell the graph takes linear space in adjacency-list form and at most cubic arithmetic work for a dense determinant. Fraction-free elimination uses polynomially many bits per intermediate integer by the minor bounds for integer matrices with bounded entries. Thus the primitive rank takes polynomial(n)·3^floor(n/2) bit operations; the constant from an odd singleton is absorbed. Only cells whose quotas sum to the prescribed z are needed for a fixed-density calculation. For the full necklace rank, a primitive length p dividing n contributes weight p and the reversal key of its (n/p)-fold repeat is multiplied by (2^n−1)/(2^p−1). Summing the corresponding strict primitive ranks over divisors changes only the polynomial overhead. The [previous colex construction](../shuffle-contraction/README.md) gives the threshold rescaling in detail.

This is an exponential improvement over scanning 2^n last columns in the BWT coordinates, and is asymptotically much slower than the archived candidate 2^{O(√n)} method in return coordinates. A cover-size bound identifies the limitation. For even n there are at least (binom(n,n/2)−n·2^{n/2})/n primitive necklaces at half density, using a union bound over proper divisors for periodic words, then dividing by n for orbits. Their primitive BWT columns are distinct. A cell consisting of disjoint switches accommodates at most 2^{floor(n/2)} columns. Even with different row pairings and with overlap, covering every such primitive column by cells of this type therefore needs at least 2^{n/2}/poly(n) cells for large even n. This bounds an explicit positive cover of the full population. A formula summing many cells at once, an estimator for a particular cut, or a different transfer representation faces a different question.

There is also a small obstruction to treating wider quota blocks as unconstrained Euler vertices. With three rows and one zero, a single vertex with three labelled loops has two tours starting at edge 0. Only one of the three stable columns has a single LF cycle. Both unrestricted tours have first itinerary bit one. Free permutation of the two same-colored targets has lost the required stability relation. Extending the construction requires enforcing precisely these local precedence relations or summing their effect.

## Reproduction and interpretation

Run `python check_joint.py` with Python 3. The oracle sorts literal rotations of binary words, constructs the last column, and bins each primitive necklace by its cell and observed reversed prefix. The test covers every prefix and cell through n=6, and every prefix observed anywhere against every cell for n=7,8. It also compares all fixed-density primitive ranks at every threshold through n=6. These 17,919 cell/prefix counts and 768 complete rank checks reproduce the recorded [output](joint-results.txt). In the order-64 cell with 32 mixed pairs, 67,108,864 of the 2^32 columns have one cycle. Requiring the initial itinerary 10101 leaves exactly 5,767,168 of them. The calculation visits a prescribed trail and evaluates a tree determinant. It constructs none of those individual columns.

The matrix-tree step uses unweighted connectivity on the contracted graph. A separate [symbolic determinant analysis](bridge.md) examines which coefficient questions on such a graph enter the earlier two-occurrence class. The reference program also checks a redundant, fully homogeneous two-occurrence lift, using a distinct diagonal symbol for each edge and distinct symbols for its tail and incidence entries. This finite check specializes the lifted determinant and compares it with explicit rooted-tree monomials on 175 seeded graphs. The theoretical scope of the link is per graph. No calculation here aggregates the dependence of the graph on an arbitrary vector of quotas. A global efficiency gain requires a way to sum the tree counts across those vectors while retaining the dependence of the trail on the requested bits.
