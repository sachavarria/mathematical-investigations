## Audit before extension

We reread and reran the [square-root bound and fixed-marker reduction](https://github.com/sachavarria/mathematical-investigations/tree/e0a61aa85ea477d1a3e53ecffcd829bb052707ec/experiments/grandmama/cut-ranking). The three archived check programs reproduced their stored results. We examined the following potential failure points.

* An overlap of exactly half the marker length is counted as long. A strictly shorter overlap forces the least finite period by Fine–Wilf. It need not divide the marker length. The short payload is its final p bits, rather than always its first p. Comparing the shifted h-factor proves strict minimality against a longer payload.
* Comparing grouped-token rotations skips the common marker. A period must map an end of a long return to another such end, since occurrences and gaps are determined by the bits. If E slots consist of E/s identical blocks, each block therefore has bit length ns/E, even when individual slots have different lengths.
* The threshold applies to the original origin. Moving u from the front to the back rotates the constraints too. The previous algorithm's all-fixed suffix and its pivot test implement this shift. Prefix-freeness makes a trie branch terminate for every surviving slot simultaneously.
* Counters are memoized per fixed transcript, fixed suffix and marker. A group has only one shared recognizer state. Its common prefix can be discarded because every unresolved edge is internal and has matched at every earlier bit. There are at most 3^E subset/split pairs times polynomially many budgets and automaton states per transcript. Fixed density adds a bounded integer budget. Counts fit polynomially many bits, including partial subproblem counts. We found no missing exponential factor in the 2^{h+O(n/h)} poly(n) estimate.
* In the 000 reduction, reversal of the forward expansion yields an ordered prefix code on reversed token keys. This works across densities even though bit lengths then differ. When querying a fixed density, one must move a general threshold to the first feasible key of that density, or handle the past-the-end case. The multiplier n/T is only applied to weighted masses of complete orbits. A fixed-density *rank oracle* is still needed, even with a known marker.
* These are internal proof checks. Neither rerunning code nor deriving a different counter at small orders constitutes external verification. The earlier square-root bound remains the best general exact bound established in this archive.

## Lexicographic machinery and its exact point of failure

Let N(x) denote the lexicographically least rotation. Weighted rank is a count of rooted words because each representative's least period is its number of distinct rotations. This orbit argument survives a change of listing order. In lex order, N(x)<a iff *some* rotation is lexicographically below a. The complement enforces a single fixed bound at every start. Hartman and Sawada's actual fixed-density algorithm normalizes the reference as needed and uses a partition DP B(t,j,d) recording length, matched prefix length and remaining density. With their necklace reference, a first larger bit clears competing suffixes that started inside the matched block. The remainder then restarts at prefix length zero. Their full algorithm handles cyclic comparisons and period/divisor terms, with cubic ranking time on a unit-cost RAM. [Hartman and Sawada, §§2–2.1 and 4](https://www.cis.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/fixDenRank.pdf)

For colex, it is the rotation selected by its *forward* comparisons whose backwards reading must pass the cut. As a concrete falsification, take x=a=0011. The strict test fails at its canonical spelling, yet its rotation 1100 passes the same colex cut. Reversal transfers the cut to lex order but selects a colex-minimal representative in the reversed orbit. Recanonicalizing that orbit alters the observation. Keeping a density budget and applying orbit inversion are valid operations. Replacing the winning rotation by an existential witness discards exactly the coupling the problem asks about.

**Exponential interval obstruction, even at half density.** For k≥0 take n=2k+10, d=n/2. Let c range over all k-block strings of blocks 01 or 10 in lex order. Put P=00001 c 1 and consider x=P0111 and y=P1101. Take threshold a=00001 (01)^k 1 1011. Each constructed word and a has exactly d ones and a unique run of four zeros at its start. Thus each is a primitive lex-minimal representative. For each c we have x<y in lex order but y<colex a<colex x, already decided by the last three bits. Each pair precedes the next pair lexicographically. Consequently the strict colex cut has at least 2^k separate included intervals in the lex listing, even restricted to fixed-density Lyndon words. This rules out decomposing the accepted set into polynomially many lex ranges or prefix cylinders. It gives no lower bound for arbitrary numerical use of a lex-rank oracle. The obstruction is also carried into the 000 test family by its expansion ψ: on these equal-density words forward lex order is preserved because ψ turns each zero run before a one into that run plus three. Colex order is preserved by its reversed prefix code. The separation persists in the lex list restricted to the encoded family.

## Exact algorithm with few minority symbols

Write m=min(d,n-d), B=n-m. If m=0 the count is immediate. If ones are the minority, encode an m-tuple of gaps of total B by concatenating 0^g 1, and order gap values from large to small. A least rotation starts at the head of a longest zero run. Starting inside a positive run loses to its head; starts with 1 lose to a zero. Among aligned starts, the ordered gap tuple therefore selects exactly the least rotation. If zeros are the minority, encode instead by concatenating 0 1^g and order gap values from small to large. Every zero is an aligned start, and the next zero distinguishes two unequal runs. These observations include zero gaps. At a tie in the counts, choose the first encoding.

In both encodings colex comparison of two *aligned* n-bit words is colex comparison of their m gaps in the same respective alphabet order. Read runs backwards until the first unequal gap. In the first encoding extra zeros win; in the second encoding an extra one loses. A comparison cannot run off the end at a first difference in the very last gap examined because the total gap sum is equal. Every aligned word is characterized by weight d and either a final 1 (first encoding) or initial 0 (second). For an arbitrary a, greedily find the first feasible reversed aligned key at least reverse(a), using remaining weight and one fixed bit. Replace the threshold by its gap tuple or use a sentinel after the last key. This works when a has another density or is not canonical. If s is the least period of a gap tuple then the least bit period is ns/m: the m/s copies have identical length, and a bit symmetry must preserve the minority occurrences and hence the gap sequence.

Generate the accepting equality/strict transcripts of the standard prenecklace extension test on m slots as in the earlier proof. This is the only necklace machinery the new exact algorithm reuses. For each transcript and each possible rightmost differing gap of the threshold, restrict that slot to values strictly smaller in the chosen alphabet order, fixing later slots to the threshold. For the sentinel impose no restrictions. Assign gap values in increasing *alphabet* order. At value v a DP state is (I,b), the set of slots already assigned and the numeric sum assigned so far. Choose a subset J disjoint from I to receive v. Enforce the cut restrictions, keep equality endpoints together, and permit a strict edge ending in J only if its predecessor is in I. Add this state's count to (I union J,b+v|J|), discarding sums above B. Read the count at the full set and sum B after the last value. Every tuple specifies its unique sequence of value classes. Conversely these transitions enforce every comparison and the sum. Sum the results of the disjoint transcripts and cut cases with weights 1, ns/m, or the indicator ns/m=n. [Classical prenecklace extension theorem](https://www.socs.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/un.pdf)

There are at most 2^{m-1} transcripts and at most 3^m pairs (I,J) per budget and value. Each count has polynomial bit length. This gives **6^m poly(n) exact bit time and 2^m poly(n) space**, including arbitrary cuts and periodic representatives. In particular all three fixed-density counts have a polynomial-time algorithm for m ≤ C log n, for each fixed C. Directly listing the minority positions can already require exp(Θ(log² n)) in that regime. At central densities the prior square-root algorithm has the better worst-case bound. Summing sparse layers alone does not give exact Grandmama time.

## Deterministic additive certificate at every density

**Statement.** For any integer K≥1, a, n and d, the weighted strict rank R has a deterministic integer enclosure L≤R≤U with U-L≤binom(n,d)/K in time polynomial in n and K. This measures additive error as a fraction of all rooted words in the layer. Exact ranking and relative error for a small rank are different requirements. The construction has conservative polynomial costs.

**Ordered variable-length dictionary.** The m gap coordinates form a weak composition of B. Independent geometric variables on 0,1,... with probabilities p_v=(m/n)(B/n)^v, conditioned on their sum being B, are uniform on these compositions. Conditioning has probability

    binom(n-1,m-1)(m/n)^m(B/n)^B
      = (m/n) Pr[Binomial(n,m/n)=m] ≥ 1/[n(n+1)].

The binomial probability at its integer mean is a mode among n+1 probabilities. Set q=1/[K n(n+1)m³], H=ceil(log₂(1/q)). When m<2H use the exact gap algorithm. This branch also costs poly(n,K) since 6^m≤6^{2H}. Otherwise grow a tree of gap prefixes, ending a branch as soon as its product of p_v is ≤q. Ignore impossible prefixes with sum>B. Every factor probability is at most 1/2, so leaf depth r≤H≤m/2. At each depth there are at most 1/q internal nodes by disjoint prefix mass and at most B+1 children each. The leaf set is polynomial in n,K. Rational probabilities have polynomial bit length. Among feasible m-tuples this is a prefix-free partition, so distinct leaves have a determined lex order in the chosen gap alphabet.

**Exact counting on the resolved portion.** For each leaf u, count anchored compositions beginning with u such that no cyclic factor of length |u| is smaller than u and u occurs just once. These conditions make this the unique least gap rotation, giving bit period n. Use a prefix matcher for u. Before appending v, reject if any currently matching proper prefix would extend by a symbol smaller than u's next symbol. Reject completion of u except at the first designated occurrence. Track the gap budget and the sign of the colex comparison; a differing later gap supersedes the earlier sign. Finally append the initial |u|-1 known symbols without charging them, to check the seam. Each fixed-leaf count takes polynomial time. Add n times the resolved accepted count to L. Add the mass of *all* unresolved words to L to get U. The two counts produced by the DP certify the width for that input even without a distributional estimate.

**Overlaps and uniform accuracy.** For r≤m/2 and two distinct cyclic starts at displacement s, put edges from i to i+s mod m for the r consecutive indices in the first fragment. This directed graph is a forest of paths: each possible directed cycle is an entire residue class mod gcd(m,s), and the complement of the r sources contains at least m/2 consecutive positions, meeting every class. For independent gaps, compare with a process drawing each path root (and isolated vertex) independently and copying its value along the path. The original probability of any matching assignment is its copying-process probability times the product of p_v over the r edge sources. This holds even with overlaps because targets are distinct and every copied edge has equal endpoint values. Hence the probability of matching fragments whose first fragment has product ≤q is at most q. Summing over the two starts and r uses fewer than m³ choices. Conditioning on sum B increases this bound by at most n(n+1). Thus the probability of any repeated dictionary leaf, and in particular a tied minimum leaf, is at most 1/K. Passing from gap tuples to uniformly rooted binary words preserves probabilities of invariant events: an orbit with s distinct gap rotations supplies ns/m distinct bit rotations. This proves the claimed width. No random sampling enters the algorithm.

**Temporal interpretation.** Sum the enclosures over all d to bound a primitive boundary's position in an interval of width at most 2^n/K. The archived polynomial backward walk transfers this to an arbitrary window, interpreting the interval on the cyclic clock when it crosses the origin. The computation is deterministic and polynomial in n,K. At a prescribed fractional resolution this gives accessible global time. At single-tick resolution the same guarantee can require exponentially large K. How to count the residual exactly, or otherwise bypass it, is still open. This theorem makes no claim that the unresolved ties are intrinsically expensive; some periodic ties have elementary answers.

The additional archive audit varies the marker scale, density and cut in 144 tests at orders 8 through 10. The new exact-rank check compares all three counts for every threshold and density through order 8 against explicit rotation orbits. It substitutes the new algorithm into the archived 000-family oracle in both directions and verifies the alternating interval construction for k=0 through 8. The certificate check tests the dictionary recurrence with deliberately coarse stopping probabilities, checks independently enumerated ambiguous mass, and exercises the small-m exact fallback. Its small instances test the dictionary separately because the conservative worst-case choice of K and n sends those instances to the exact branch. A large-order end-to-end performance claim is outside these checks.

For the displayed order-14 query the exact triple is (2900,208,207) from a layer of 3432 rooted words. With q=1/128 the dictionary gives [2884,2900]. Those endpoints are computable without the exact rank. Example q is a diagnostic choice; it is not the conservative q specified for arbitrary inputs.
