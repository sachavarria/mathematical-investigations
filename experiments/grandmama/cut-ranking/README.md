# Counting through a colex cut without enumerating return schedules

Working mathematical note, 26 November 2026. Research question by S.A. Chavarría. Derivation and reference implementation by Codex (GPT-6). Agent 404's reproduction concerned earlier findings. This is a candidate original mathematical contribution pending independent verification and confirmation of publication priority.

## Statement

For any binary word a of length n ≥ 1, let W consist of the length-n words equal to their least lexicographic rotation whose reversals are strictly lexicographically below the reversal of a. Write per(w) for the number of distinct rotations of w. There is a deterministic algorithm computing |W|, the number of primitive words in W and the sum of per(w) over W in time and space bounded by 2^{O(√n)} bit operations and bits, respectively. All three counts may optionally be restricted to a specified Hamming weight. A need not be a necklace. No precomputed table is assumed.

The [preceding algorithm](../return-ranking/return-codes.md) gave a time bound of 2^{O(√(n log(n+1)))}. Its minimum-marker enumeration survives in this algorithm. Its enumeration of return schedules, simultaneous automaton states and complete weak orders can all be eliminated. The resulting bound is still superpolynomial. These are comparisons between upper bounds.

## Proof

**1. Read from the end of a minimum factor.** Fix 1 ≤ h ≤ n. Partition the target words by their first h bits u. A representative starts with its least cyclic h-factor. Mark the starts of all copies of that factor, including overlapping copies. To compare marked starts, skip their identical prefix u and compare their forward periodic streams. From the end of each copy to the end of the next copy one reads a nonempty elementary return payload.

Here is a polynomial-size recognizer for the payloads at given u. Its state q is the length of the longest suffix of the text that is a prefix of u, initially h. For each k<h such that u[:q] ends in u[:k], including k=0, reject a next bit c if c<u[k]. Update to the longest prefix of u that is a suffix of u[:q]+c. Arrival at h ends an elementary return. This is a deterministic prefix-free code. Every cyclic factor smaller than u has a first differing bit, which triggers rejection. Conversely every rejection gives such a witness on a periodic stream. For the converse construction, repeat any accepted concatenation that begins and ends at h and has total length n. After n ≥ h symbols the state depends only on the stream itself. Its terminal h symbols are u, so the cyclic context at the seam agrees with the initial state. Reanchoring h places earlier recovers the word beginning with u.

**2. Group forced repetitions into single tokens.** Call a return short if its length d satisfies 2d<h. Let p be the least ordinary period of the finite word u. An overlap at short distance d makes d a period, with p ≤ d. The Fine–Wilf length condition holds since p+d ≤ 2d < h. Thus gcd(p,d) is a period and p divides d. The two overlapping copies together are p-periodic in the same phase. If d>p a further copy begins at p, contradicting first return. Every short return therefore equals the same word S, the final p symbols of u.

Moreover S, when available, is lexicographically smaller than *every* long return. At displacement p within u the h-factor starts with u[:h-p]. The ensuing p bits must be at least S, or the shifted factor would be smaller than u. Equality completes a copy at distance p, before the long return could finish. Thus long returns differ upwards within those first p bits. This argument also works on any accepted long payload because its length is at least ceil(h/2)>p when a short return exists.

If all returns are short, the only possibility for this u is the length-n repetition of u[:p], with p dividing n and 2p<h. Test canonicality and the threshold directly. Conversely such a repeated canonical word has minimum prefix u, shortest bit period p, and a mark every p bits. Otherwise group the circular elementary stream into tokens consisting of zero or more short returns followed by one long return. They form a prefix-free code because completion of the first long return is deterministic. A token recognizer adds to q only the age since the last elementary return, capped at ceil(h/2), resetting that age at each short return and stopping at a long return.

The minimum marked start must be a group boundary. From the head of a run of short returns the stream has more initial copies of S than from any internal mark in that run, and at the first difference S beats the long return. Every nonmarked start loses on its first h bits. At the remaining starts the comparison after u is exactly comparison of the circular tuple of grouped tokens in their ordinary lex order, by prefix-freeness. Thus a tuple anchored at its least token rotation gives precisely one canonical binary spelling. Tied rotations spell the same tuple. There are E ≤ n/ceil(h/2) tokens. If their least token period is s, their first s tokens have total bit length ns/E, which is a bit period. Conversely every bit-period shift preserves marks and the long-return gaps between them, hence preserves group boundaries and induces a token-period shift. This proves that the least bit period is ns/E.

**3. Record only the comparisons used.** For E fixed, generate symbolic transcripts of the prenecklace extension test, with zero-based token indices. Start at j=1, k=0. Require token k ≤ token j. In the equality branch increment k; in the strict branch reset k to zero. Increment j in either branch. At j=E keep the transcript iff s=E-k divides E. The standard extension theorem says the current prefix is repetitions and a prefix of its longest Lyndon prefix of length j-k. Matching the next periodic symbol retains this length, increasing it starts a new Lyndon word, and decreasing it invalidates the prefix. The divisibility test therefore accepts exactly the necklaces and s is their least token period. The theorem applies to this ordered token alphabet since only finitely many tokens can occur at length ≤n. Each tuple takes one transcript, and there are at most 2^{E-1} transcripts with at most E-1 equality or strict-comparison edges each. [Cattell et al., Theorem 2.1](https://www.socs.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/un.pdf)

**4. Impose an arbitrary ambient cut.** Write w=uV, so the token stream of total length n is P=Vu. The strict colex cut breaks into disjoint suffix tests. For each index j ≥ h with a[j]=1, require P to end in 0 followed by a[j+1:] followed by u. This specifies the rightmost differing bit of w. When V=a[h:], also accept the exact stream a[h:]+u if the reverse of u is smaller than the reverse of a[:h]. These tests include h=n. For a fixed required suffix v, its first bit lies in a unique token, the pivot. Guess the number b ≥ 1 of suffix bits in that token. The pivot must end in v[:b]. Parse v[b:] deterministically from a token boundary and require it to be a list of whole tokens, fixing the final slots exactly. Each matching assignment has exactly one such b. This removes the need to know individual token lengths in advance.

**5. Count a transcript by branching on shared prefixes.** The following finite dynamic program sums over all variable-length token assignments of total length n. Its state consists of a subset G of the E labelled slots still sharing a prefix, its depth, their shared token recognizer state, the pivot suffix match length when relevant, and B, their total remaining bit budget. The fixed slots and comparisons are parameters of this transcript and cut. Initially all slots are together at depth zero, with B=n. A terminal group accepts once iff B=0, there is no internal strict edge, all fixed lengths agree with the depth and the pivot suffix test succeeds if present. Prefix-freeness ensures that all slots at such a node terminate together. The empty group contributes one exactly at zero budget.

Otherwise partition G into the slots receiving bit 0 and those receiving bit 1. Charge |G| bits. Enforce each fixed bit at this depth, forbid splitting either end of an equality edge, and forbid a strict edge's smaller endpoint receiving 1 when its larger endpoint receives 0. Discard illegal recognizer transitions on occupied branches. An edge separated in the allowed direction is settled forever. Split the remaining budget between the two children, multiply their counts and sum. A suffix matcher on the pivot branch updates by the usual longest suffix-prefix rule. Optional fixed density uses a second total budget, charging the size of the 1-child and splitting the remainder. Rotation by h preserves this density.

Induction on remaining length proves the recurrence. Every assignment uniquely specifies its split and the two children's budgets. Conversely the two child assignments concatenate their new bits to the consumed prefixes, with every cross-child comparison already decided. Memoization needs no text of the shared prefix. Its effects on future acceptance are exhausted by the local recognizer, depth, pivot matcher and the comparisons still internal to G. The number of subset/split pairs is at most 3^E, with polynomially many other state values, budget allocations and operations per pair. Counts have polynomial bit length, for example by bounding partial assignments by length compositions and at most n freely specified bits. Summing over transcripts, suffix choices, E and 2^h markers takes 2^{h+O(n/h)} poly(n) bit operations. The sequentially reused space satisfies the same bound. Take h=ceil(√n). Add the all-short case separately, weighting each accepted tuple by its proven bit period when computing the third statistic. This completes the proof.

**Temporal consequence.** At a primitive boundary its position in the Grandmama concatenation equals the weighted strict count. The earlier archived decoder walks backwards by the inverse PCR2 rule to such a boundary in O(n) steps and adds the walked distance modulo 2^n. Substituting this counter gives the same improved upper bound for arbitrary binary windows under that construction's origin convention. The local boundary-distance theorem is inherited from the archive. The new component is the global count. [Previous decoder and references](../return-ranking/README.md)

## The split family already contains the global ranking question

For marker 0^h (h≥3), restrict elementary returns to 0 and 1 followed by h zeros. Let T be the number of returns, t the number of long ones, 0<t<T, and n=T+ht. Define ψ on token bits by 0 ↦ 0 and 1 ↦ 0^h1. Writing φ for the elementary return encoding, 0^h φ(q)=ψ(q)0^h, so ψ gives the spelling at the marked origin. For a least token rotation q, ψ(q) is the least bit rotation. Indeed consider starts of the circular zero runs preceding ones, allowing zero-length runs in the token spelling. The least rotation starts at a longest run, with ties resolved by comparing successive run lengths, larger first. Expansion adds h to every run length and preserves all these comparisons. Internal starts in an expanded run lose to its head. The number of repeated copies of a primitive root is also preserved, since periods permute the ones and the circular sequence of run lengths, whose rotational symmetries are unchanged by adding h. Thus per(ψ(q))=(n/T)per(q).

On reversal the expansion is a prefix code sending 0 to 0 and 1 to 1 followed by h zeros. It strictly preserves lex order of all length-T keys, even across different token densities. For any ambient threshold b, binary search the reversed T-bit keys for the first expanded reversal ≥ reverse(b), with a sentinel past the end. The family mass is n/T times the weighted strict rank of that token threshold restricted to density t. At the sentinel the token mass is binom(T,t). This gives 2^{O(√T)} poly(n) time even when b splits the family and is outside the image. The inversion of the cut alone is polynomial.

Conversely suppose an oracle computes this family's arbitrary-cut mass in polynomial time with the marker fixed at 000 and parameters T,t supplied. Given any T-bit threshold a, in each density 0<t<T find the least reversed T-bit key of weight t at least reverse(a). This is polynomial by greedily extending a prefix using feasibility by remaining length, weight and threshold comparison. If it exists let q be the reversal of that key, query at ψ(q), and multiply the family answer by T/(T+3t). Otherwise take the full layer mass binom(T,t). This works even if q is not canonical, since the colex embedding acts on all words. Sum the layers and add the two constant words when below a. This polynomial-time Turing reduction computes unrestricted weighted colex rank. It pinpoints what a polynomial split-family formula would accomplish even with a known constant marker. It gives no hardness classification.

## Verification and scope

Run with Python 3 from this directory. The scripts use only the standard library. Keep assertions enabled.

    python3 check.py
    python3 family_check.py
    python3 phase_check.py
    python3 cut_rank.py 00010111

The first script constructs literal binary rotation orbits independently of the counter. It compares all three statistics for every threshold through n=7 and eight selected thresholds per order from 8 to 11. It separately checks every binary word and every marker length through n=11 for short-return minimality, actual tokenization, agreement of minimizers and equality of periods. The second script checks fixed-density weighted ranks and arbitrary ambient cuts including cuts outside the expansion image, against direct bit orbits. The third substitutes the new counter into the archived decoder and compares every window through n=7 with a separately concatenated clock. The exact outputs are included. These are finite checks and an internal proof audit. No machine formalization or independent verification of the new proof is claimed.

The unresolved mathematical issue is whether counts for different minimum fragments or different comparison states admit a polynomial summary that composes under a strict colex cut. A complete family sum or an efficient successor rule alone does not give that summary. The fixed-marker reduction shows why removing *only* marker discovery leaves a problem with the full general weighted rank as a polynomial-time consequence. It leaves other representations entirely open. The grouping and subset algorithm make no claim about an intrinsic cost of inference, a practical speedup at small orders or the difficulty of perceptual recognition.
