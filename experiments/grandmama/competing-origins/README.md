## Setup

The population consists of binary words of length n, optionally of density d. The label of a rotation orbit is its lexicographically least spelling w. At threshold a the requested bit is reverse(w)<reverse(a), strictly. Multiplying this bit by the least bit period counts all rooted words of that orbit. The binary Grandmama temporal reduction uses precisely this weighted rank at a primitive boundary and a polynomial local walk from an arbitrary window. Global general exact ranking remains open.

In a nonconstant circle a least rotation starts at a run head, a 0 preceded by 1. At depth h keep the run heads whose forward h-prefix is least. A *cell* fixes that minimum prefix u. Let r be its number of candidate occurrences on a circle, counting repeated occurrences in periodic words. From the end of the common u read successive first-return payloads to the end of the mark 1u, forbidding a run head with smaller prefix. These form a prefix-free code. There is a polynomial-size equivalent matcher retaining the longest suffix that is a prefix of the mark or a forbidden pattern. All forbidden patterns have length at most |1u|. The context after each return is known, including at overlaps. Repeating any accepted sequence of returns supplies a consistent cyclic context since sufficient repetitions determine the matcher state. At a candidate origin the future after u is this token sequence. Comparing its cyclic rotations in lexical token order selects the least bit rotation. Other starts cannot win. Constant circles are handled separately.

## I. FPT in the number of occurrences

**Theorem.** For a specified u and arbitrary a, all accepted weighted masses at multiplicities 1 through R can be calculated in 6^R poly(n) bit operations and 2^{O(R)} poly(n) space. The exponent on n does not depend on R. This is a parameterized counting theorem about a population. A promise about the observed window alone need not constrain the circles lying below its rank.

**Proof.** At multiplicity e, use the classical prenecklace extension test on the e ordered payload slots. At slot j compare to slot k, initially j=1 and k=0. An equality increments k, a strictly greater new slot resets k to zero, and a smaller new slot rejects. Increment j. At the end retain s=e-k dividing e. This is the standard extension criterion with longest Lyndon prefix length j-k. It gives one accepting comparison transcript per canonical tuple and at most 2^{e-1} transcripts. The least token period is s, hence the bit period is ns/e. A bit period preserves the mark and hence the token boundaries; a token period repeats identical spells. Tied spellings are counted once. [Cattell et al., Theorem 2.1](https://www.socs.uoguelph.ca/~sawada/papers/un.pdf)

Place the weight only after anchoring the tuple at its winning orientation. Write w=uV; the stream of e payloads is P=Vu. For every j≥h with a[j]=1 the rightmost difference there prescribes the suffix 0 a[j+1:] u of P. Also admit the exact P=a[h:]u when reverse(u)<reverse(a[:h]). The disjoint tests encompass strict arbitrary thresholds. In a required suffix v, guess the number b of its leading bits that are the ending of its first touched slot (the pivot). Parse the remainder deterministically into complete trailing slots. There is one valid b per accepted assignment. This keeps the backward question attached to the selected origin without storing e backward answers.

The archived trie DP can now run with the elementary-return recognizer in place of the grouped-return recognizer. At a node, all its slots share the bit prefix read so far. Partition the labelled subset into next-bit zero and one groups. Equal endpoints must stay together, a smaller endpoint cannot branch to 1 when its larger endpoint branches to 0, and every permitted separated comparison is settled. Pass down a shared matcher state, depth, a total remaining bit budget, and a pivot suffix matcher only on its branch. Fixed trailing slots prescribe their bits at each depth. Split the budget and multiply the child counts. Prefix-freeness forces a terminal node to end all its slots together, with pending strict comparisons rejected. Induction on remaining budget proves the count because every assignment supplies exactly one partition and allocation. A density budget is split the same way. For one transcript there are at most 3^e subset/split pairs times polynomially many scalar states and allocations. Summing over transcripts and e proves the bound. The rank contribution of each accepted tuple can alternatively be weighted by 1 or by the indicator ns/e=n for ordinary and primitive necklace counts.

**Using the slices in a certificate.** The earlier lower and upper endpoints classify circles by unanimous or possible yes. Subtract their contributions at e≤R and insert the exact canonical yes mass at these multiplicities in both endpoints. Compute what to subtract with the earlier cyclic avoidance trace, marking each occurrence of reverse(u)1 by a variable y. Retain coefficients y^1 through y^R. The suffix-pattern automaton tracks occurrence and forbidden outputs on the same transition, including overlaps. A second variable can track density. Thus the resulting width is *exactly* the rooted mass with more than R candidates and both signs present. A homogeneous prefix cell can be closed early; every descendant already agrees.

For the unrestricted population its width at uniform depth h and R≥1 is at most 2^n binom(n,2) 2^{-h}/R. Each unresolved circle has at least R opposite-sign unordered pairs: split e≥R+1 candidates into two nonempty classes, whose size product is at least e-1. For a fixed pair to disagree, its h-fragments must match but its rotations must differ. As proved in the preceding note, either the h equality edges form a forest (matching probability 2^{-h}) or they force equality of the complete rotations. Sum over pairs and divide by R. This trades counting effort for a stronger upper bound; neither a general time lower bound nor an improved general exact exponent follows.

## II. Polynomial summaries for arbitrarily many origins

**Unique minimum theorem.** Fix u. The complete accepted mass of circles whose least outgoing payload occurs once is computable in polynomial time, summing over any number of origins and over all possible bit spellings of that minimum. This includes a single candidate. Such circles are primitive. The condition permits all other payloads to repeat freely.

**Proof.** Anchor the least payload M at slot zero. Precisely the strict comparisons M<X_i are needed. Apply the suffix decomposition from I. Remove the known trailing slots, charging their lengths and ones. M must be less than their smallest known spelling if any. Keep M and the pivot explicitly; they may coincide. Every other surviving slot has the same role: a word larger than M. In a trie node with f such labelled slots, choosing j of them to take zero and f-j to take one gives binom(f,j) assignments to those branches. If M takes one, no competing slot can take zero. An internal tie with M is not allowed at a terminal. The fixed-word wall on M needs just a comparison sign and depth; the pivot needs the suffix-match length. Counts, lengths, depths, and budgets range up to n. The number of distinguished roles is constant. Summing over f and over all trie splits is polynomial. As in I, a unique winning anchored tuple specifies every accepted necklace once. This proves the claim and explains why a histogram can replace labelled subsets **only after the colex suffix has been attached**. See unique.py.

**Bounded frequency theorem.** More generally, fix the frequency t≥1 of the least elementary payload M, while letting the total multiplicity range up to r. The exact accepted mass of that slice is computable in (r+1)^{O(t)} poly(n) bit time for r≥2. Thus a fixed frequency gives polynomial time at unbounded total multiplicity. This is an XP bound in t. The FPT bound in I has a different parameter. The reference code uses r=floor(n/2), the maximum possible number of run heads.

**Proof.** Write the circular token sequence in t blocks, each consisting of M followed by an ordered tail of tokens strictly larger than M. A winning rotation starts at M. Comparing two block tails in ordinary list lex order gives the comparison of the stream at the first differing block. When one tail is a proper prefix, the shorter one encounters M while the longer still sees a greater token. The empty tail is allowed. The transcript test of I therefore operates on t ordered block symbols. A block period s gives bit weight ns/t since any bit period also preserves the minimum token starts.

Enumerate the lengths of the t tails **in token slots**, up to r total slots, in (r+1)^{O(t)} ways. For each of at most 2^{t-1} accepting transcripts, expand each required comparison of two tails. Equality identifies corresponding slots throughout, requiring equal lengths. A strict comparison either has a specified first differing slot or is a proper-prefix comparison. Identify the matching initial slots and, for the former case, impose one strict inequality at the differing pair. There are at most (r+1)^{t-1} such joint choices. They are disjoint for any given assignment. Identifications between nonminimum slots always stay in the same column measured from the start of their respective tails. Thus any resulting nonminimum equality class has multiplicity at most t. There are at most t-1 exceptional strict edges beyond the comparisons against M.

Impose the colex suffix on the expanded sequence of elementary slots just as in I. Remove classes pinned to fixed spellings, rejecting inconsistent pins; charge all their occurrences, including any occurrences outside the fixed suffix. Collapse the constraints M<fixed to one upper wall. Retain M, the pivot if unpinned, and the O(t) endpoints of exceptional edges as labelled variables. Edges to removed classes become unary comparisons against known spellings. Each remaining ordinary class of multiplicity j needs only the condition that its spelling exceeds M. Keep a vector (c_1,...,c_t) counting these classes by multiplicity. For each split choose b_j of the c_j classes for the zero child, weighting by the product of binom(c_j,b_j). A class contributes j copies of every bit to the budgets. Check the same order, terminal, and pivot conditions as before. Track comparisons to O(t) fixed walls by three-way signs and common trie depth. The induction from I applies to labelled classes reconstructed by the binomial factors. The census vectors and their splits cost (r+1)^{O(t)} and the distinguished masks and comparison signs cost 2^{O(t)}. All other factors are polynomial in n with absolute exponent. The sum over all shapes, transcripts, suffix choices and cases gives the stated bound. blocks.py implements the construction, including nonprimitive ties, optional density, fixed minimum, and a pivot shared with other occurrences. No enumeration of the bitstring M is required.

**A failed summary.** At n=8, density 4 and threshold a=00011101 (reversed key 184), two canonical spellings have these return words to 10 and reversed candidate keys in forward order:

| w | payloads | keys | winner below 184? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00101011 | 010, 10, 110 | 212,154,166 | no |
| 00101101 | 010, 110, 10 | 180,150,210 | yes |

Both have the same multiset of payloads, the same unique least payload, and two yes votes among three candidates. These data cannot identify the winning sign. The polynomial algorithm retains the placement of the terminal suffix relative to M before pooling the remaining roles. Conversely enumerating every labelled subset can be extravagant: in the all-strict transcript at u=0 choose the least slot 0010, and give the remaining slots payloads 010 or 10. All are legal. At fixed counts there are binomially many first-bit label subsets, all satisfying the same comparisons. This describes a cost of the labelled representation; the symmetric recurrence above eliminates it on precisely this family.

## III. A closed-form shallow-depth exactness profile

This section concerns the *original cut-sensitive binary certificate*, with run-head candidates, unrestricted density and no algebraic resolution of ties. Let N=2^n, k=int(reverse(a),2), and W_h(k) its integer width. For a circle, write l,b for the extreme reversed keys of its candidates. Its width contribution is its orbit size times the indicator l<k≤b. This is the definition used throughout, including at endpoints.

**Theorem.** Put H=max(2,h). If n≥4H+4, define L=N/2+2^h and U=N-2^H-2. Then

    W_h(k)>0  if and only if  L<k≤U.

In particular every threshold outside that single interval is exact at this depth. Among keys in the upper half of the key space the number with zero width is 2^h+2^H+2, independent of n in this regime. The theorem determines the support, not the height of the width curve. It makes no assertion about a fixed-density layer or the multiplicity-refined certificate. The sufficient depth condition has not been optimized.

**Necessity at the lower edge.** A candidate in a nonconstant circle starts at 0 and ends in 1, so its key is ≥N/2. If its key is strictly below L, all ones in its forward spelling except the final one lie among the first h bits. If there are none, there is only one run head. Otherwise a run of at least n-h-1≥h zeros precedes the final one, while the candidate prefix contains a one. Such a start cannot be minimal. Hence a circle with distinct candidate keys has minimum key ≥L.

**Necessity at the upper edge.** A candidate key is even. If it exceeds U, its missing-bit mask N-1-key is odd and at most 2^H. Hence all zeros in the forward spelling lie at indices 0 through H-1. For h≥2, another candidate would start at some zero j>0 and match the h-prefix. Let z<h be the last zero. Its match would require a zero at z+j, strictly later and without wraparound. This is impossible. At h=1 the only possible second zero is adjacent to the first and cannot be another run head. Thus distinct candidates have maximum key ≤U.

**Covering every interior threshold.** Three circles suffice. The two run heads in 0^h 1 0^(n-h-2) 1 both attain the minimum prefix, with keys L and A=N/2+2^(n-h-2). In the word with zeros precisely at positions 0 and H and ones elsewhere, both heads share the minimum prefix, with keys B=N-2^(n-H)-2 and U. Finally construct

    0^h 1^(H+1) 0^h 0^(h+2+padding) 1

with padding chosen for total length n. This exists under the stated condition. Both displayed run heads begin in 0^h. The backward reading at the first starts with 1 followed by at least h+2 zeros, giving a key strictly below A. At the second it starts with H+1 ones, giving a key strictly above B. Thus the three half-open spans cover (L,U]. The earlier necessity arguments forbid any additional mixed keys. This proves the characterization. The exhaustive tests also found an actual internal gap, keys 4161 through 4192 at n=13,h=5, outside the theorem's regime: a single-interval assertion for all n,h would be false.

## IV. Measuring uncertainty over all questions

Define S_h=sum_{k=0}^{N-1} W_h(k). The exact orbit identity is S_h=sum_X per(X)(b_X-l_X). The following deterministic aggregate estimate works without enumerating the N queries. Write the endpoints as lower(k),upper(k); both are nondecreasing cumulative counts and equal N at a formal sentinel k=N. On a grid x_i=floor(iN/J), 1≤J≤N, compute both endpoints. At the left endpoint of each bin its width is exact. At each remaining interior key the width lies between max(0,upper(x_i)-lower(x_{i+1})) and upper(x_{i+1})-lower(x_i). Multiply by the interior bin size and sum. The total uncertainty in S_h is at most 2N(ceil(N/J)-1) by telescoping the increments of both cumulative counts. This bounds the normalized all-question area S_h/N² to additive interval width <2/J for nondegenerate J. It gives the exact sum on the full grid. We do not have a polynomial-time exact all-depth area algorithm.

The cost is polynomial in n,J uniformly in h. First use the collision bound W_h(k)≤N binom(n,2) 2^{-h}, capped at N, and multiply by N (or by the proved support size). If this is already within the requested area tolerance, stop. Otherwise enumeration to depth h in the endpoint oracle costs polynomial in n,J: when J≤N/2, ceil(N/J)-1≥N/(2J), so failure of that bound forces 2^h=O(n²J). When J>N/2, even 2^n<2J. Positivity on the proved support gives a free integer lower bound. These are additive guarantees; they can be weak relative to a tiny area. The flag counts and opposite-pair bound in I also control the integrated width after R-way refinement.

## Reproduce and interpret

Place this directory beside the archived cut-ranking and question-sensitive-ranking directories. Python 3 and NumPy are required. All prior files remain in their original directories.

    python check.py
    python block_check.py
    python examples.py
    python composition.py 00000110110111 3 4

The rotation oracle enumerates independently of all return recognizers and verifies both interval endpoints, exact mass vectors, and minimum-frequency slices. Frozen outputs are in checks.txt, block-results.txt and examples.txt. Finite verification does not establish the asymptotic proofs. At the unrestricted order-14 example the depth-three widths at R=1,2,3,4 were 1435,315,49,0; the exact weighted rank is 12666. This differs from the earlier density-seven rank 2900 because the populations differ. At order 24 the unique-minimum branch at u=0 accounted for 14,906,208 rooted words out of 16,777,216; that is an exact population size at one order, not a limiting frequency. The bounded-t prototype prioritizes reproducibility over speed.

The remaining question is whether the census of equality-class multiplicities and exceptional comparisons can itself be summed symbolically when both the number of origins and the least-payload frequency grow. The present construction proves that large populations of competitors can have polynomial descriptions, and gives explicit cases where the order/answer association must survive compression. It establishes no intrinsic lower bound on the cost of recovering the clock.
