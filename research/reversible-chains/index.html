How to read the study

The two chains use identical starting populations. Each site keeps or flips its bit according to a function of its two left neighbors. The chain has two fixed zeros beyond the left boundary. Its rule number encodes four output bits, from the least significant bit for neighbors 00 to the most significant for 11.

In a response section, every possible assignment of the unread cells occupies a common angle in cyclic Gray order. Radius encodes the answer, increasing with its integer value. For a two-bit observation the earlier listed bit is the high bit. A group of readings with identical answers makes one shell; axial thickness measures the fraction of readings in that group. The gap between shells separates distinct response functions. Contours interpolate between discrete samples. Drag sideways to turn both objects together.

Evolution shows an individual trajectory. Time runs down the drawing. Across it, each curve follows a cell. Bright marks are ones; dim marks are zeros. The connecting curves retain cell identity. A line along the upper edge marks the cells already read. Reading order changes which initial cells have been observed. It leaves the dynamics intact.

With nine cells and rule 4, a change in the order of reading can be seen without changing the evolution. At time seven, read all five even sites first, then compare with all four odd sites first. Open the nine-cell comparison.
