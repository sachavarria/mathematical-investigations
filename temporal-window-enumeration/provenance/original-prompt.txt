Frontier Mathematical Research Experiment: Cryptographic Foundations

Your objective is to produce an original, independently verifiable mathematical contribution to the foundations of cryptography within the computational resources available in this session.

Prioritize the following interconnected domains:

* Computational complexity and information recoverability
* Reversible computation under partial observability
* Mathematical foundations of one-way transformations
* Information-theoretic security
* Novel cryptographic constructions and formal security guarantees
* Relationships between temporal inference and computational irreversibility

Phase I: Autonomous problem selection

Conduct a rigorous literature and novelty audit. Identify at least five genuinely unresolved, narrowly formulated mathematical problems for which meaningful progress might be achievable through conceptual reasoning rather than extensive computational search.

Evaluate each candidate according to novelty, tractability, mathematical significance, available verification methods and its potential to reveal a more general mathematical principle.

Select the strongest candidate independently and explain your decision.

Phase II: Original mathematical investigation

Develop your own conjectures, definitions, intermediate results and proof strategies.

Investigate alternative mathematical representations of the problem. Examine whether changing its representation exposes structures that are difficult to recognize in its conventional formulation.

Prioritize conceptual compression, structural insights and unexpected relationships between mathematical domains.

Use computational experiments on synthetic systems where appropriate, particularly to discover counterexamples and test conjectures.

Phase III: Adversarial verification

Attempt to invalidate your own results.

Identify hidden assumptions, boundary cases, counterexamples, unjustified generalizations and possible equivalences to existing theorems.

Where feasible, produce a machine-checkable formalization in Lean 4.

Phase IV: Research output

Produce:

1. A precise statement of the strongest original result.
2. A complete proof or explicitly identified partial proof.
3. Reproducible computational evidence, where relevant.
4. A source register establishing the current state of the literature.
5. An assessment of novelty and mathematical significance.
6. A candid account of unresolved issues.
7. The most promising subsequent research question.

Conduct this investigation using abstract mathematics and synthetic experimental systems, without targeting deployed cryptographic infrastructure.

A rigorous partial result is preferable to an unsupported claim of a major breakthrough. Do not manufacture novelty.

Use your available reasoning resources as fully as possible. Treat the identification of a valuable research problem as itself part of the intellectual task.
